\title{The Regularizer That Switched Off the Experiment:\\ Why Our Protein-Interaction Null Was\\ About the Encoder, Not the Prior}

\author{Vijayavallabh Jayamanikandan \\ Indian Institute of Technology Madras \\ Chennai, India \\ \texttt{be23b041@smail.iitm.ac.in}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We asked whether a typed protein-interaction prior improves prediction of how primary human T cells
respond to genetic perturbation, and report two failures. First, a textbook edge-sparsity
regularizer, written as an unnormalized sum over the edges of each sampled neighborhood, is about
$103\times$ the response term at its default weight and drives every learnable edge gate to
$\sim$$10^{-7}$ within the first epoch, in all five seeds. Training still completes and reports a
plausible score, so a graph-versus-no-graph comparison silently becomes no-graph versus no-graph.
Second, with the gates live, the evidence-gated graph is indistinguishable from an expression-only
baseline ($\Delta\,\textsc{systema}=-0.0009$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0072,+0.0054]$, $n{=}5$; not significant after family-wise correction), and the typed graph's null holds across seven further Perturb-seq screens. The only
contrast in that five-seed family to clear correction failed to replicate on a harder split. Of five
candidate causes, the two that carry our argument concern our encoder and our baseline, not biology. The
baseline was never graph-free: zeroing its graph-derived features costs $-0.0036$ \textsc{systema}
under correction, while the gated message passing over eight million typed edges that we set out to
measure has no detectable effect (the two do not differ significantly). And an untyped diagnostic arm, which keeps the raw topology but drops the typing and gating,
beats the baseline on our own screen ($+0.0043$, $n{=}7$, surviving Bonferroni and Holm) and on
Replogle RPE1 ($+0.0675$), and \emph{loses} to it on Norman ($-0.0790$): three of eight datasets
clear correction, in \emph{disagreeing} directions. Our null is therefore evidence about the encoder we
built to exploit the prior, not against the prior: a ``graph versus no graph'' null can be an artifact
of the encoder. The null is also limited by the instrument: with a known graph-dependent signal
injected, the gated arm clears correction at no size up to $0.40$ response SDs, where the untyped arm
clears at $0.02$, though neither arm clears on the increment over its own un-injected level. We close with
the checklist we wish we had used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Genome-scale Perturb-seq now measures the transcriptional response to knocking down nearly every
expressed gene in primary human cells
\citep{dixit2016perturbseq,replogle2022genomescale,zhu2025gwperturbseq}, and protein-interaction
networks encode decades of curated mechanism. Does giving the network to a model help it predict the
response to a target it has never seen? Published answers disagree (\S\ref{sec:related}).

We built a model to give the graph its best chance: an evidence-gated, typed protein-interaction
encoder (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}), tested as a pre-registered nested ablation with paired seeds and
family-wise error control. Most of the project went into discovering that our instrument was broken.
We report:

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Perturbation-response prediction.} Benchmarks report that deep and foundation models do
not beat simple linear or mean baselines \citep{ahlmanneltze2025linear,wongmoccia2025crispr} and that
tabular foundation models match specialized architectures \citep{hollmann2025tabpfn,royer2026tfm};
other work reports that graph and foundation models win under their own splits and metrics
\citep{wenkel2026txpert,cole2026foundation}. The disagreement tracks split design and metric choice
more than architecture. \textsc{gears} \citep{roohani2024gears} and TxPert \citep{wenkel2026txpert}
build gene-gene knowledge graphs into the predictor, the design choice we test. \textsc{systema}
\citep{vinas2025systema} evaluates predictions beyond the systematic variation that perturbations
share; scPerturb \citep{peidli2024scperturb} harmonizes public screens and compares them by
E-distance. Appendix~\ref{app:metrics} re-scores our null under that and four other external
endpoints.

\paragraph{Interaction priors and edge masks.} Relational graph convolutions
\citep{schlichtkrull2018rgcn} keep one message function per edge type and a GCN \citep{kipf2017gcn}
ignores types; our arms use both, and our architecture search adds attention \citep{brody2022gatv2}.
PINNACLE \citep{li2024pinnacle} compresses a context-specific interaction network into a protein
embedding, which can carry graph information into a model without message passing (inert here;
\S\ref{sec:causes}, D). Our condition gate is closest to differentiable edge masking
\citep{schlichtkrull2021graphmask}, which also learns a per-edge gate under a sparsity penalty, so the
confound of \S\ref{sec:confound} is a hazard of that construction in general, not of this model alone.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Problem}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Task and data.} For each (perturbation, condition) pair we predict, in a latent program
space, the transcriptional response to knocking down the target gene. The data are the genome-scale
CRISPRi Perturb-seq screen of primary human CD4$^{+}$ T cells of \citet{zhu2025gwperturbseq} (four
donors; conditions Rest, Stim8hr and Stim48hr). The supervised target is a differential-expression
matrix of $33{,}983$ (perturbation $\times$ condition) rows over $10{,}282$ genes, covering $11{,}526$
distinct gene targets, modeled in a $K{=}128$ sparse-PCA program basis \citep{zou2006sparsepca} fit
\emph{inside} each training fold.

\paragraph{Metric and split.} The primary endpoint is \textsc{systema}, the perturbation-specific
delta-correlation of \citet{vinas2025systema}, which asks whether a prediction recovers the
\emph{correct} perturbation's response profile rather than a generic average treatment effect. The
headline split is biologically blocked target-OOD: targets are grouped by centered ESM-2 sequence
similarity (cosine $\geq 0.85$, group size capped at $5\%$), which leaves $24\%$ of validation targets with a close training paralog, against $60\%$ for a naive split (Appendix~\ref{app:folds}), and every
response-derived transform is fit inside the training fold.

\paragraph{Statistical protocol.} Five paired seeds share one frozen fold. Four contrasts are
pre-registered (Table~\ref{tab:arms}), and each must clear \emph{both} Bonferroni and Holm
\citep{holm1979}; a benefit must also be positive at $n\geq4$ paired seeds, a bar fixed before any
training run. The results rest on $379$ training lanes (single training runs) with early stopping on validation loss; Appendix~\ref{app:repro} gives the compute and schedules.

\subsection{Proposed approach}
\label{sec:approach}

\paragraph{Graph.} Nodes are proteins and protein complexes, joined by $\sim$$8$M typed edges:
physical PPI \citep{huttlin2021bioplex,luck2020huri,oughtred2021biogrid}, co-complex
\citep{tsitsiridis2023corum} and functional association \citep{szklarczyk2023string}, each with a
confidence score. Functional associations are $85\%$ of the edges (Appendix~\ref{app:causes}), the tier a skeptic would suspect of adding noise (\S\ref{sec:causes}, E).

\paragraph{Model.} EG-IPG (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}) represents the target by frozen features only, with
no trainable gene-ID embedding, so it must generalize to unseen genes. A typed encoder passes signed
messages over a sampled $2$-hop neighborhood and scales every edge by a learnable gate conditioned on
culture context and the edge's provenance features. A learned mixture $\gamma$ combines the graph prediction with
an expression-only one, and $\gamma{=}0$ recovers the no-graph arm exactly (Appendix~\ref{app:arch}).

\paragraph{The nested family.} Four arms differ only in how the graph enters, so every contrast is an
ablation inside one architecture (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). h1, the gated graph against no graph, is the
hypothesis the model was built for and the subject of the headline null. The approach can work only
if curated interaction evidence carries co-regulation information that expression alone does not,
and if the gate can suppress the edges that carry none. \S\ref{sec:confound} shows the second
precondition failing silently.

\section{Results: observed outcome}
\label{sec:outcome}

\subsection{The instrument was off}
\label{sec:confound}

Our first multi-seed comparison found the graph inert: edge gates sat near $10^{-7}$, and the graph
arm was indistinguishable from the no-graph arm. We recorded a clean negative result. A later
gate-health probe showed that the graph had been switched off before it learned anything.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{lambda_sweep.pdf}
\caption{The default edge-sparsity weight silently disables the graph. \emph{One} seed per run,
twenty-two epochs on the first $512$ training and $128$ validation rows of the blocked fold
(Appendix~\ref{app:archsearch} explains why one seed suffices). (a) Final mean gate and fraction of
gates dead; dotted, the default $10^{-2}$; band, the five scored full-data lanes at
$\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}{=}0$ (at the default, full-data gates sat at $\sim$$10^{-7}$). (b) Gate per epoch, dashed at the dead-gate threshold $10^{-3}$: weights
from $10^{-3}$ up are dead from the second epoch, $10^{-5}$ from the twelfth; none recovers.}
\label{fig:lambda}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Mechanism.} The training objective carries an edge-sparsity and low-confidence penalty
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{graph}}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{r,b,e}\big(|\alpha^{r}_{b,e}| + (1-\mathrm{conf}^{r}_{b,e})(\alpha^{r}_{b,e})^2\big),
\label{eq:graphloss}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha^{r}_{b,e}$ is the gate on edge $e$ of relation $r$ in the neighborhood sampled for batch
element $b$, and $\mathrm{conf}^{r}_{b,e}$ is that edge's confidence. The sum runs over \emph{every
edge of each sampled neighborhood}, tens of thousands of them, and is divided by the batch size $n$,
never by the edge count, so the effective penalty changes with the sampler. At the default weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}{=}10^{-2}$ the term is
$\approx$$103\times$ the mean-reduced response term. AdamW \citep{kingma2015adam,loshchilov2019adamw}
scales each parameter's step by that parameter's running gradient magnitude, so a penalty this large
plausibly fixes the \emph{direction} of the gate updates, not only their size. The sparsity term,
meant as a mild prior, becomes the objective.

\paragraph{Signature.} Every gate collapses to $\sim$$10^{-7}$ within the first epoch, in all five
seeds. In a sweep the final gate falls monotonically with $\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}$, and no penalized
run recovers (Figure~\ref{fig:lambda}). The run does not crash, warn or diverge, and it
reports a plausible \textsc{systema}.

\paragraph{Why it is dangerous.} With the gates collapsed, the graph arm \emph{is} the no-graph arm, so
the graph-versus-no-graph contrast compares a model with itself and returns a small difference with a
tight interval. It fails in the direction we were prepared to believe: we expected a null, and got one.

\paragraph{Fix and precondition.} The fix that worked is one line: set $\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}{=}0$.
Normalizing the penalty per edge does not rescue the gates, as the direction argument predicts: in a
pilot $98\%$ were dead after one epoch. Removing the penalty raised the mean gate by a factor of
$4.5\times10^{6}$, from $\sim$$10^{-7}$ to a live $0.27$ to $0.78$ in the checkpoints we scored. It also moved the headline from $-0.0019$ to $-0.0009$ (h1, \S\ref{sec:null}), so the repair did not manufacture the null. The dead-gate run's parity with no graph means nothing on its own; we use it only as a control on the fix. We now read the gate mean
before any result: below a dead-gate threshold ($10^{-3}$) the graph comparison is undecidable by
construction, and the run is rejected. The three-minute check decides whether a multi-hour run
means anything.

\subsection{With the instrument working, the graph still does not help}
\label{sec:null}

graph is statistically indistinguishable from expression-only: its interval crosses zero and it clears
neither correction. The static graph, with every typed edge forced on, is worse than no graph on this
fold, the only contrast in the family to clear correction.

\paragraph{The null survives a second metric, a harder split and a third fold.} Re-scored under
program-space Pearson-delta, which does not subtract the training mean, both conclusions hold
(Table~\ref{tab:family}, last column; $p=0.64$ for the gated graph), with program cosine agreeing
($-0.0005$, $p=0.65$). Pearson-delta has under half the paired standard deviation of
\textsc{systema}, so it has \emph{more} power, yet it still finds nothing for the gated graph while
clearing the arms Table~\ref{tab:family} stars. A harder split and a third fold at an intermediate
similarity threshold give the same corrected verdict at different point estimates
(Figure~\ref{fig:floor}a; \S\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Our first significant contrast did not replicate.} The harder split also tests whether h2a
replicates, and it does not (Figure~\ref{fig:floor}a). At the same $n{=}5$ the static graph's deficit
there is $-0.0012$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0074,+0.0050]$, $p=0.61$, clearing neither; it shrank elevenfold,
and one seed reverses sign. h2b and the untyped margin shrink the same way
($+0.0122\!\to\!+0.0017$, $+0.0045\!\to\!+0.0028$), and on the harder split \emph{nothing} in the
family survives correction. On the third fold h2a's estimate holds but its seed noise more than
triples (Appendix~\ref{app:folds}), so ``typed static graphs hurt'' is a statement about one split.

\subsection{The null replicates, but it is about the encoder}
\label{sec:repl}

We replicated the comparison on seven further Perturb-seq screens \citep{peidli2024scperturb}
(Figure~\ref{fig:repl}) spanning four cell types beyond the reference's, both perturbation directions
and a hundredfold range of target counts, each with its own blocked target-OOD split, program basis
refit inside its training fold, four seeds per arm and family correction (Appendix~\ref{app:repl}).

\paragraph{The typed null holds, with a bound.} Pooled over the seven replication datasets the typed
static graph is worth $+0.0018$ \textsc{systema} (fixed-effect $95\%$ CI $[-0.0005,+0.0040]$; random
effects $[-0.0028,+0.0065]$; $I^2{=}39.2\%$, $Q$ $p{=}0.13$). Heterogeneity across the seven is not
significant despite that spread, and the genome-wide screen, the best powered, is the tightest of them.
Frangieh, the only dataset where the gate has anything to gate, is underpowered rather than null
(Appendix~\ref{app:repl}).

\paragraph{The exception points at our encoder.} On Replogle RPE1 the \textsc{untyped\_gnn} arm beats the expression-only baseline
by $+0.0675$ \textsc{systema}, all four seeds positive ($p{=}0.009$, Bonferroni and Holm both $0.019$ at family size two), while the typed arm on the same dataset goes the other way (Figure~\ref{fig:repl}).
Where the graph does help, our typed encoder does not show it.

RPE1 is not the only case. On our own screen the untyped arm is $+0.0043$ \textsc{systema} at $n{=}7$
(all seven seeds positive, Bonferroni $0.027$ and Holm $0.020$), where the typed static arm loses (Figure~\ref{fig:causes}a). Across the eight datasets, however,
three clear family-wise correction and \emph{they disagree in sign}: the reference and RPE1 gain,
Norman ($-0.0790$) loses (Figure~\ref{fig:repl}b). Norman's result does not rest on one seed: all four
of its seeds are negative and none was dropped. Pooled over the eight, random effects give $+0.0091$
($p{=}0.12$) at $I^2{=}87.5\%$, post hoc because the registration pools only h1 and h2a, and close to
uninformative at this heterogeneity; neither cell type nor perturbation direction predicts the sign. The
$K{=}128$ pooling, equally post hoc here, gives $+0.0141$ ($p{=}0.0056$), but neither survives the small-sample
Hartung-Knapp adjustment ($p{=}0.24$ and $0.48$; Appendix~\ref{app:repl}). Our
encoder did not miss a weak signal; it flattened a strong and inconsistent one, which flips cause C
(\S\ref{sec:causes}) from refuted to surviving.

\paragraph{C. The architecture is wrong.} We first recorded this cause as refuted on a $14$-cell
architecture search whose single-seed, five-epoch cells, untyped ones included, could not resolve these
differences (Appendix~\ref{app:archsearch}). Running the whole family at $n{=}7$ on the frozen fold locates
the damage instead (Figure~\ref{fig:causes}a): the typed static encoder does it, and the condition gate
repairs part of it, unreliably, rather than adding signal. Most of that damage is not about evidence types: normalizing each relation's
messages by degree recovers $79\%$ of the untyped gap (a family of one, uncorrected $p{=}0.0245$) yet
still does not beat no graph, so the deficit looks like an aggregation artifact. Under the shared
stopping rule the typed arms are scored by epoch $3$ and the others at $13$ to $19$, so part of the typed arms' cost may be how early those models peak. \textbf{Survives}; it best explains the null
(Appendices~\ref{app:mechanism} and~\ref{app:causes}).

\paragraph{D. The baseline was never graph-free.} The \textsc{expression\_only} arm, the usual no-graph
baseline, turns message passing off, but its frozen target features already carry graph-derived
information: three PPI degree scalars and a $128$-d PINNACLE embedding.
Zeroing those as well costs $-0.0036$ under correction, while gated message passing over roughly eight million
typed edges (h1), the quantity the paper set out to measure, has no detectable effect
(Figure~\ref{fig:causes}b). Whether the summary is worth \emph{more} than h1 is not settled: paired by seed it comes out ahead in all five seeds, but its interval crosses zero. An ablation that labels one arm ``no graph'' while feeding it a graph summary
does not measure what its name says. \textbf{Survives} (Appendix~\ref{app:causes}).

\subsection{Boundary conditions}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The bound is per fold.} No fold yields a gated-graph benefit that survives correction, and
no single fold's upper limit bounds the effect elsewhere (Figure~\ref{fig:floor}a;
Appendix~\ref{app:folds}).

\paragraph{Detection limits the gated arm, not the untyped one.} At $n{=}4$ the gated arm clears at no injected size up to $0.40$, the largest we injected,
while the untyped arm clears from $0.02$ but not on a scrambled graph; on the increment over its own
zero point, neither clears (Figure~\ref{fig:floor}b; Appendix~\ref{app:floor}). Our headline null therefore describes what this encoder can resolve at this budget, not whether a PPI prior helps.

\paragraph{Our pre-registration nearly prevented the replication.} Under the rule as written, four of six candidate datasets fell out, held to a standard the reference data do not meet (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). Under the amended rule two narrower limits remain: h1 rests on a single replication, and no dataset replicates the reference \emph{cell type} (Appendix~\ref{app:repl}).

\paragraph{Scope.} Gains reported elsewhere \citep{wenkel2026txpert,cole2026foundation} use different
splits and metrics, so our bound constrains this task rather than adjudicating those claims. Under
their endpoints our null holds, but the untyped contrast \emph{reverses sign} between TxPert's and
\textsc{gears}' metrics, and by gene rank the gated arm clears correction in the graph's
favor on the least-moved genes (Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}).

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to test whether a protein-interaction prior helps predict T-cell perturbation responses,
and the harder problem was trusting our own measurement: a conventional regularizer had switched off
the mechanism under test. With the gates repaired, the gated graph is indistinguishable from no graph,
the typed static graph's null holds across seven further screens, and the one contrast to clear
multiplicity control failed its own replication. The untyped arm, run only as a diagnostic, clears
correction on three datasets in disagreeing directions, a sign that neither cell type nor perturbation
direction predicts and that we cannot cleanly separate from program capacity, so our null is evidence
about the encoder, not against the prior. A graph ablation should also strip the usual ``no graph''
baseline's graph-derived features, and a negative result about a prior should test its simplest
encoder. For us that was one extra arm; without it we would have published the wrong cause and, had we
stopped at five datasets, a sign that the sixth reversed.

\section{Model architecture}
\label{app:arch}

EG-IPG has four modules: a perturbation and context encoder, a typed evidence-gated graph encoder, a program decoder with a graph/expression mixture gate, and a sparse rationale head in the GNNExplainer lineage \citep{ying2019gnnexplainer}, fitted after the predictor is frozen (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).

\paragraph{Perturbation and context encoder.} The target protein is represented by frozen features only, with \emph{no} trainable gene-ID embedding, so the model cannot memorize target identity and must generalize to unseen genes: ESM-2 650M ($1280$-d) \citep{lin2023esm2}, PINNACLE ($128$-d) \citep{li2024pinnacle}, three PPI degrees and control-derived baseline expression, $1412$-d in total. These are fused with a condition embedding and donor context into an intervention vector $h_{\mathrm{do}}\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$. This vector, and therefore the \textsc{expression\_only} arm, contains graph-derived features (\S\ref{sec:causes}, cause D). Coverage over the $33{,}983$ DE rows: ESM-2 resolves for $99.4\%$, physical and functional PPI degree are nonzero for $96.5\%$ and $98.4\%$, complex degree for $29.5\%$, and PINNACLE for $9.2\%$ (the embedding store returns a zero vector on a miss).

\paragraph{Typed evidence-gated graph encoder.} Around each target we sample a $2$-hop neighborhood capped at $512$ nodes and run $L{=}3$ relational message-passing layers \citep{schlichtkrull2018rgcn}. For an edge $u\!\to\!v$ of relation $r$,
\begin{equation}
m_{u\to v}^{(l),r}=\tanh\!\big(W^{(l),r}_{\mathrm{sign}}h_u^{(l-1)}\big)\odot\mathrm{ReLU}\!\big(W^{(l),r}_{\mathrm{mag}}h_u^{(l-1)}\big),
\end{equation}
a signed message whose $\tanh$ factor encodes activation or inhibition and whose $\mathrm{ReLU}$ factor encodes magnitude. The condition gate
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{u\to v}^{r}(c)=\sigma\!\big(w_{\mathrm{gate}}^{r}\cdot[\,h_{\mathrm{cond}}(c)\,\|\,f_e\,]+b_{\mathrm{gate}}^{r}\big)\in[0,1]
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
is applied before aggregation, so the same physical edge can be active in one context and silent in another. Unlike graph attention, it is an independent per-edge sigmoid conditioned on culture context and the edge's provenance features, not a query-key similarity normalized over a neighborhood. Nodes update residually, and a $4$-head cross-attention readout queried by $h_{\mathrm{do}}$ pools node states into $h_{\mathrm{graph}}\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$.

\paragraph{Decoder and mixture gate.} The decoder predicts a program delta from the graph and a parallel expression-only prediction that bypasses it, then mixes them with a learned $\gamma$. Setting the graph encoder to null forces $\gamma{=}0$ and recovers the \textsc{expression\_only} member exactly, so the family is a nested ablation rather than a comparison of two code paths.

\paragraph{The search could not have answered its question.} Four of its cells drop the typing and gating (the untyped rows of Table~\ref{tab:archsearch}), so it could in principle have found cause C, but it was underpowered. Every cell is one run at one seed, and our rule emits no $p$-value at $n{=}1$, because a single seed is not a paired result, so no cell was individually decidable, and the search as a whole distinguished nothing. Across all $14$ cells the best is $+0.0051$ against the no-graph baseline and the worst is $-0.0037$, a spread of $0.0089$, while $14$ draws of the \emph{seed noise alone} on the frozen fold ($\mathrm{sd}=0.0043$) would be expected to span $0.0147$. The whole search is narrower than what re-seeding a single configuration produces, and its best cell would need $12$ paired seeds to clear our correction rule at $80\%$ power. Its budget was also biased. Every cell ran \emph{five} epochs. In our full-length lanes the typed arms reach their best validation loss by epoch $3$ and the untyped and no-graph arms not until epochs $13$ to $19$ (Appendix~\ref{app:causes}). If the inner fold trains alike, five epochs catch the typed cells at their best and the rest well short of theirs. That budget favors the cells that came out on top, and nothing here separates convergence speed from final quality. We ranked $14$ architectures on differences we could not resolve, at a budget unlike the one we report, and then read the ranking as a refutation.

\paragraph{Why Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} may be single-seed when this table may not.} The $\lambda$ sweep behind Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} is also one run per point, so the objection above applies to it on its face. We accept it there for a reason that does not transfer. That figure measures a gate collapsing across seven orders of magnitude, from $0.76$ to $\sim$$10^{-7}$, and the largest seed-to-seed spread we measure anywhere in this paper is a factor of two on a quantity of order $10^{-2}$. The architecture search, by contrast, spans differences of $0.0089$ against a seed spread of $0.0043$, the regime where one seed decides the ranking. The sweep clears its own noise by a margin re-seeding cannot close; the search does not.

\section{Which part of the typing does the damage?}
\label{app:mechanism}

\S\ref{sec:causes} localizes the deficit to the typed encoder: $-0.0120$ \textsc{systema} at $n{=}7$, seven of seven seeds, surviving both corrections. Two explanations are confounded inside that number. The relation partition may be the wrong inductive bias; or typed message passing carries four times the message parameters over the same edges, in which case the damage is capacity and has nothing to do with evidence types. Two arms separate them (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}), each registered with its decision rule before either was trained.

\textsc{typed\_shared} ties one message module across all four relations instead of one each, cutting $2{,}396{,}160$ message parameters to $599{,}040$ and the model's total parameters by $34\%$ ($5{,}254{,}884$ to $3{,}457{,}764$), and changes nothing else. \textsc{typed\_permuted} keeps the per-relation modules and reassigns every protein-protein edge's relation label at random, preserving each relation's edge count exactly. The relabeling happens \emph{after} neighborhood sampling, because the sampler ranks candidate neighbors by relation: permuting the stored graph would have changed which neighbors enter the subgraph, not only which weights process them.

Neither explains the deficit. Cutting the message parameters fourfold, a third of the whole model, moves the primary endpoint by $+0.0004$; if the deficit were capacity or overfitting, that intervention would have fixed it. Replacing the true relation partition with a random one of identical sizes moves it by $+0.0065$, which does not clear correction. That estimate is \emph{positive}, so if anything the curated partition is slightly worse than a random one, but under our rule it is a null and we report it as one.

What remains is what all three typed arms share and the untyped one does not. Both diagnostic arms sit well below the untyped \textsc{gcn} ($-0.0172$ and $-0.0111$) and below the no-graph baseline ($-0.0126$ and $-0.0065$), so the deficit survives both interventions. The remaining candidates concern the form of the message rather than its routing: signed messages, the per-edge feature term, the complex-membership node type, the residual feed-forward block, and summing a relation's messages instead of normalizing them by degree.

We had reported the last of these as refuted. The architecture search that refuted it spans $0.0089$ across all $14$ of its cells, while the untyped-minus-typed gap we measure at $n{=}5$ on the frozen fold is $0.0176$. A search whose whole range is half the difference under study was not resolving these arms, so ``refuted within the searched space'' was never established for normalization either.

\paragraph{Degree normalization recovers most of the deficit.} \textsc{typed\_gcnnorm} is the static typed encoder with each relation's messages weighted by $1/\sqrt{d_i d_j}$ instead of summed. Nothing else changes, not even the parameter count, since the parameter-sharing arm had already excluded capacity. Against the \textsc{typed\_static} lanes at the same five seeds it recovers $+0.0139$ \textsc{systema} ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0029,+0.0248]$, five of five seeds positive), or $79\%$ of the $+0.0176$ gap to the untyped arm. Most of the deficit is therefore the unnormalized per-relation sum, not the relation labels: a relation's contribution scales with its degree, so \textsc{functional\_assoc} ($85\%$ of all edges, median score $0.209$) dominates the node update by count.

\paragraph{It is not a graph benefit.} First, repairing the encoder buys nothing against no graph: \textsc{typed\_gcnnorm} is $+0.0008$ against the expression-only baseline ($[-0.0047,+0.0062]$, $p=0.71$, three of five seeds) and cannot be told apart from the untyped arm ($-0.0037$, $p=0.22$). Fixing the aggregation removes the damage and reveals the null of \S\ref{sec:null}, with one fewer artifact in front of it. Second, this is a family of \emph{one}, where Bonferroni and Holm are the identity, so ``clears both corrections'' means only the uncorrected $p=0.0245$; the three other registered message-form arms would make $m{=}4$, where it does not clear. What survives multiplicity is the sign agreement and the size of the share.

\paragraph{Why we stopped at one arm.} The residual the other three would partition is $\textsc{untyped\_gnn}-\textsc{typed\_gcnnorm}=+0.0037$, against a minimum detectable effect of $0.0096$ at five seeds, $2.6\times$ the whole residual, and larger still against any one arm's share. Detecting the residual needs about $21$ seeds, and half of it about $77$, so three more arms at five seeds would have been underpowered by construction. Stopping also keeps the family at one and so preserves the $p$ above, but the power calculation, not the $p$, is why we stopped.

\section{Replication protocol and dataset survey}
\label{app:repl}

We pre-registered the multi-dataset replication before running it: the contrast family and its correction, the seed count, the primary contrast per dataset, kill criteria and integration rules. The accompanying dataset survey bears on how any such replication should be read.

First, few candidates approach the reference's target count. The reference screen has $11{,}526$ gene targets. One harmonized public alternative \citep{peidli2024scperturb} is comparable, the Replogle K562 genome-wide screen at $9{,}730$ built targets, and it gives the tightest interval of the seven (\S\ref{sec:repl}). The rest are far smaller: the largest is Replogle RPE1 at $2{,}122$, the largest with more than one experimental condition is Frangieh melanoma at $238$ over three conditions, and the two T-cell candidates, Shifrut in primary cells and Datlinger in the Jurkat line, yield $20$ and $30$. Because the split holds out sequence-similarity families, target count sets how many families exist to hold out, so outside the genome-wide screen a replication is a weaker measurement than the reference.

Second, target count and condition count are anti-correlated across candidates. Every dataset with enough targets for a well-powered split has a single condition, and there \textsc{condition\_gated} degenerates to \textsc{typed\_static} because its condition embedding is indexed by a constant. On those datasets h1 is undefined and the primary contrast must be h2a; the pre-registration assigns it per dataset in advance, not after seeing results.

\paragraph{Measured outcome.} Ten candidates were built under the corrected DE rule, eight passed on-target quality control, and seven were trained to completion: Frangieh melanoma, Replogle RPE1, Replogle K562-essential, Replogle K562 genome-wide, Norman K562, and Tian iPSC-neuron CRISPRi and CRISPRa (Table~\ref{tab:repl}). The two that failed quality control, Datlinger and Shifrut, were the pool's only T-cell data, which is why the replication spans four cell types but not the reference's own. The program dimension is not portable: $K$ cannot exceed the training fold's row count, and a replication dataset has one row per (target, condition), so the reference $K{=}128$ does not hold everywhere. Tian CRISPRi runs at $K{=}32$, and Tian CRISPRa and Norman at $K{=}16$, each a labeled deviation fixed before the first lane ran (Table~\ref{tab:repl}).

\paragraph{The $K{=}128$ subset disagrees with the full pool.} Amendment 3.1 requires the pooled estimate over the $K{=}128$ subset beside the one over all datasets (\S\ref{sec:repl}) and fixed in advance that where the two disagree, the subset speaks to the reference architecture. We report both estimates and resolve neither; the registration pools only h1 and h2a, so for the untyped arm both are post hoc. Over all eight datasets the untyped margin is $+0.0091$ by random effects, which govern at this heterogeneity ($[-0.0024,+0.0207]$, $p{=}0.12$); over the five $K{=}128$ datasets it is $+0.0141$ ($[+0.0041,+0.0240]$, $p{=}0.0056$), with all five positive. Heterogeneity stays high in the subset ($I^2{=}87.7\%$), and the only negative dataset, Norman, is one of the three that ran below $K{=}128$, so the sign disagreement is not cleanly separable from an eightfold cut in program capacity. Capacity does not explain it either. The other two sub-$128$ datasets carry the second and third largest positive effects (Tian CRISPRi $+0.0281$, CRISPRa $+0.0226$), and one negative among three against none among five arises easily by chance ($p{=}0.375$, Fisher exact). Nor is either estimate robust to the small-sample Hartung-Knapp adjustment, which the registration did not specify and we report only as a sensitivity analysis: it widens the five-dataset interval to $[-0.0139,+0.0421]$ ($p{=}0.24$), the eight-dataset one to $[-0.0201,+0.0384]$ ($p{=}0.48$), and the typed arm's seven-screen random-effects bound of \S\ref{sec:repl} to $[-0.0053,+0.0089]$.

\paragraph{On-target validation.} Because an adapter can produce a matrix that is well shaped but wrong, we validated the surviving multi-condition dataset against biology it cannot fake: in Frangieh, knockouts reduce their own transcript (own-gene $\log_2$FC mean $-0.613$, $90\%$ negative, against $-0.002$ and $63\%$ for random genes).

\section{Folds, re-draws and the per-fold bound}
\label{app:folds}

\paragraph{How blocked the frozen fold is.} Blocking by sequence family reduces train-validation paralogy; it does not remove it. The split's own audit measures only the training set against the sequestered split. Run between training and validation targets, the same function finds $24.0\%$ of validation targets ($356$ of $1{,}482$) with a training target at centered cosine $\geq 0.85$ (maximum $0.996$), against $60\%$ for a naive split that deals out every twentieth gene. The non-chaining clustering that keeps groups from merging into one giant family is what lets some above-threshold pairs land in different groups.

\paragraph{A harder split.} Lowering the sequence-similarity threshold from $0.85$ to $0.75$ and raising the family-size cap from $5\%$ to $15\%$ blocks more paralogs and lowers the median train-to-sequestered-split sequence cosine from $0.796$ to $0.741$. We compare only paired within-split contrasts across folds: the validation sets differ in size and composition, so absolute \textsc{systema} is not comparable between splits even when the contrast is, and the re-draws below show why we do not treat that cosine as a clean difficulty axis either. The gated graph stays at parity ($\Delta\,\textsc{systema}=+0.0005$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0105,+0.0115]$, $p=0.90$, $n{=}5$ paired seeds, per-seed differences $-0.0003$, $+0.0035$, $+0.0032$, $-0.0138$, $+0.0100$). The point estimate here is nominally \emph{positive}, unlike the frozen fold's $-0.0009$, and we read nothing into that: the interval straddles zero almost symmetrically, the contrast clears neither correction, and one seed ($-0.0138$) shifts the mean by more than the mean's own size. The sign is undetermined at this budget, and the harder split gives a wider interval, not a tighter bound.

\paragraph{A third fold.} Two folds invite a story about difficulty, so we ran a third at an intermediate threshold (Table~\ref{tab:folds}). The corrected h1 verdict is the same on all three, indistinguishable, but the point estimates are not: $-0.0009$, $+0.0082$, $+0.0005$. On the intermediate fold all five seeds favor the graph and the \emph{uncorrected} interval excludes zero ($p=0.025$); it fails family-wise correction ($p_{\mathrm{Bonf}}=0.101$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.101$), so the verdict stands, but reporting only the two folds whose estimates sit near zero would be selective. h2a failed to replicate twice, for different reasons. On the intermediate fold h2a is $-0.0122$, within $7\%$ of the frozen $-0.0131$, yet clears no correction ($p=0.23$) because the across-seed standard deviation is $0.0169$ there against $0.0049$ on the frozen fold. On the harder split the estimate itself collapses to $-0.0012$ ($p=0.61$). The per-seed differences show why: $-0.0010$, $-0.0013$, $-0.0033$, $-0.0070$, $+0.0066$. One seed reverses sign. Only the second failure, the collapse on the harder split, says anything about the effect.

\paragraph{One split specification, three re-draws.} Three folds are not three difficulty levels. Holding threshold and cap fixed at the intermediate setting and varying only the partition seed, three realizations of the \emph{same} split specification give median train-to-sequestered-split cosines of $0.759$, $0.793$ and $0.862$. Partition noise alone spreads the statistic over $0.103$, against $0.056$ for the entire designed range across all three thresholds ($0.796$ to $0.741$): re-drawing one split moves the difficulty statistic almost twice as far as deliberately changing the threshold does, and the third re-draw is \emph{easier} than the frozen fold it was meant to sit below. The realizations also differ structurally, sharing only $20\%$ of their validation targets and differing twofold in validation size, and the no-graph baseline moves with them: at five paired seeds per re-draw it scores $0.0991$, $0.0942$ and $0.0845$ \textsc{systema}, a $0.0146$ spread on our most stable arm, larger than any effect in Table~\ref{tab:folds}. It does not track the cosine statistic, since the nominally hardest re-draw scores highest. It orders with validation-set size, which the re-draws moved from $3{,}632$ to $7{,}216$ unbidden, though with three re-draws that ordering would arise by chance one time in six, so we offer it as the more plausible reading rather than a measured relationship.

The contrast moves too. Running the graph-versus-no-graph comparison on all three re-draws at five paired seeds each gives h1 $=+0.0082$ (uncorrected $95\%$ CI $[+0.0017,+0.0148]$, $p=0.025$), $+0.0026$ ($[+0.0005,+0.0047]$, $p=0.027$) and $+0.0021$ ($[-0.0024,+0.0065]$, $p=0.266$). The experiment, the specification and the five seeds are the same and only the partition draw differs, yet the estimate spans fourfold and \emph{the qualitative verdict flips}. Two re-draws give an interval excluding zero at $p\approx0.03$ and the third does not, so an author who drew the first would report an apparent graph benefit, and one who drew the third would report nothing. What survives all three is the sign and the correction: none clears Bonferroni over the pre-registered family of four ($0.101$, $0.109$, $1.000$). That is why the corrected verdict carries the paper and the uncorrected intervals serve as a cautionary exhibit.

Each contrast is valid within its own split, but the \emph{ordering} in Table~\ref{tab:folds} carries far less information than three rows suggest. We therefore do not claim a dose-response in OOD difficulty, and we suggest the same caution for any single-realization split sweep: a threshold knob that appears to control difficulty may move it less than the partition seed does. Establishing a difficulty effect requires several realizations per level, so that between-level differences can be read against the within-level spread.

\paragraph{The nested variance decomposition.} We then ran that design. Three re-draws at $0.75/0.15$ and a fourth difficulty level at $0.70/0.05$, five paired seeds each except the frozen fold's seven, give six cells over four levels and split the nested variance three ways: between levels, between re-draws within a level, and between seeds within a re-draw. For h2a the components are $\mathrm{sd}_{\text{seed}}=0.0107$ ($26$ df), $\mathrm{sd}_{\text{level}}=0$ ($3$ df) and $\mathrm{sd}_{\text{redraw}}=0.0003$ ($2$ df). For h1, over five cells at three levels, they are $\mathrm{sd}_{\text{seed}}=0.0057$ ($22$ df), $\mathrm{sd}_{\text{level}}=0.0027$ ($2$ df) and $\mathrm{sd}_{\text{redraw}}=0.0023$ ($2$ df). These figures were corrected after review (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

Partition noise looks \emph{contrast-dependent}: h1's re-draw component is $0.0023$ against h2a's $0.0003$, roughly sevenfold, as the flipping verdicts above suggest. The two estimates are not measured on the same partitions, though. h1's three re-draws sit at the intermediate $0.80/0.10$ setting and h2a's at the harder $0.75/0.15$, because those are the levels at which each contrast's arms were replicated, so the sevenfold difference is confounded with difficulty level: it may be the contrast, or it may be the level. h2a shows no difficulty effect at all: once seed and re-draw noise are subtracted its level component is zero, so its four level means differ by no more than that noise predicts. The term that dominates both contrasts is the seed. For h2a the training seed is the only component distinguishable from zero, and it is the only one here with enough degrees of freedom to be estimated precisely; for h1 the seed leads the difficulty setting by $2.1\times$, with $2$ df behind the level term. A split sweep read off single-seed runs is measuring the training seed.

\paragraph{Per-fold upper limits.} The load-bearing quantity is each fold's interval, and it bounds that fold only. The per-fold upper limits are $+0.0054$ (frozen), $+0.0148$ (intermediate) and $+0.0115$ (harder), so across folds the data are compatible with roughly three times the frozen-fold ceiling. No fold, and no re-draw of a fold, yields a gated-graph benefit that survives family-wise correction over the pre-registered family of four, and no single fold's upper limit bounds the effect elsewhere.

\section{The candidate causes in full}
\label{app:causes}

\paragraph{A. The task is close to null for everyone.} Centroid accuracy sits at floor ($\approx$$0.001$) for every arm, and all four trained arms occupy a band from $0.072$ to $0.090$. An independent tabular benchmark reports this exact screen as a near-null-signal regime in which models barely separate from the perturbed mean \citep{royer2026tfm}. If there is little perturbation-specific structure to recover, no prior can route it. That does not excuse the graph, but it bounds how much any component could have contributed, and it is why we report a \emph{bounded} null rather than a claim of zero.

\paragraph{B. The metric is not hiding a gated-graph benefit.} \textsc{systema} is deliberately conservative about average-treatment-effect shortcuts, so it could also suppress a real gain. Under Pearson-delta, which has more power (\S\ref{sec:null}), h1 stays null while the untyped arm and the gate over static typing clear correction, so the metric can hide a benefit, but not the one the headline is about. Refuted for h1; Appendix~\ref{app:metrics} shows how far the other contrasts move with the endpoint.

\paragraph{E. The tier we would have pruned dominates the rationale.} Functional associations are $85\%$ of the edges and the low-confidence tier. Pruning them, the obvious hygiene step, lowered \textsc{systema} and the gates in the architecture search, but no single-seed cell there is decidable (Appendix~\ref{app:archsearch}); in a live-gate rationale audit, ablating that tier degrades sufficiency most ($\Delta=0.365$, against $\leq0.0021$ for BioPlex, HuRI and CORUM). If the graph is mostly noise with its signal in its least confident edges, an encoder that sums every edge without normalization pays for that noise (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}). Ours can switch edges off, which may be why it reaches parity rather than the static arm's deficit, but gating recovers only enough to break even.

\paragraph{The audit's ratios, at their sizes.} Every number in that paragraph compares against a matched control, which is the right design but yields a ratio, and in a near-null regime a ratio can be large while the quantity it divides is negligible. Three corrections follow, all against us. First, STRING is $85.4\%$ of the graph's $8{,}029{,}296$ protein-protein edges, against $1.5\%$ for BioPlex, $1.2\%$ for CORUM and $0.6\%$ for HuRI; the remaining $11.3\%$ is BioGRID, which the audit did not ablate. Ablating STRING therefore removes most of the graph. Per $1\%$ of edges removed it costs $0.0043$ sufficiency against HuRI's $0.0025$ and BioPlex's $0.0014$, a lead of $1.7$ to $3\times$ rather than the two orders of magnitude the raw deltas imply; only CORUM stays far behind, at $0.00002$. Second, on sufficiency the rationales do \emph{not} beat matched random rationales of the same size. Sufficiency is a distance, so smaller is better, and the rationale's is larger by $+0.011$ on a scale of $1.47$, or $0.8\%$ ($95\%$ CI $[+0.006,+0.016]$, $n{=}50$, $p{=}2.6\times10^{-5}$); it beats random in $4\%$ of cases. Third, on necessity the rationales beat their control by $49\%$, but both quantities are of order $10^{-7}$, so ``$92\%$ of cases beat random on necessity'' is true and nearly empty: deleting the selected edges moves the prediction by about one part in ten million. The one comparison with a substantive gap is GInX \citep{amara2023ginx}, $0.084$ against random's $0.030$ at $20\%$ sparsity, and as a sufficiency distance it points the same way: the rationale is the less sufficient of the two. Cause E therefore stays at \emph{partly}: that tier dominates the loss of sufficiency largely because it holds most of the edges, and the rationale itself is no more sufficient than a random one of the same size.

\paragraph{C: the intervals behind the arm ordering.} Ordered by \textsc{systema}: untyped $0.0904$, expression-only $0.0861$, condition-gated $0.0825$, typed-static $0.0740$. At $n{=}7$ on the frozen fold, the typed static encoder costs $-0.0120$ \textsc{systema} ($95\%$ CI $[-0.0160,-0.0080]$, Bonferroni and Holm both $0.001$); the condition gate returns $+0.0085$, whose raw interval excludes zero but which does not survive correction (Bonferroni $0.186$), leaving the gated arm at $-0.0036$ against no graph ($95\%$ CI $[-0.0093,+0.0022]$); and the untyped arm is $+0.0043$ ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0017,+0.0069]$, paired $p=0.0068$).

\paragraph{C: when each arm is scored.} Early stopping on validation loss (patience $10$, cap $20$ epochs) is shared by every arm, and it ends them at very different epochs. On the five-seed frozen fold the no-graph and untyped lanes run to the cap with best checkpoints at epochs $14$ to $19$ and $13$ to $19$; the static typed lanes stop after $11$ to $13$ epochs with the checkpoint from epoch $0$ in four of five seeds, and the live-gate gated lanes after $11$ to $14$ with checkpoints from epochs $0$ to $3$. The typed arms are therefore scored as models that peaked, by validation loss, within their first four epochs. The rule is the same for every arm, so this is not a difference in budget, but we did not test whether a longer schedule, or selection on the endpoint rather than the loss, would change h2a or h1.

\paragraph{D in full: what the ``no graph'' baseline contains.} The frozen feature vector that arm carries is graph-derived in two places: three PPI degree scalars, whose physical and functional components are nonzero for $96.5\%$ and $98.4\%$ of rows (medians $53$ and $604$), and a $128$-d PINNACLE embedding \citep{li2024pinnacle} learned on a cell-type-contextualized interaction network. The contrast we and others label ``graph versus no graph'' therefore measures \emph{the marginal value of message passing over a node-level graph summary}, and the ablation is blind to that summary by construction.

A complete version of the ablation is three-way, also stripping graph-derived node features, and we ran it (Appendix~\ref{app:ablate}): zeroing them, with dimensionality and parameter count unchanged, costs $-0.0036$ \textsc{systema} over five paired seeds under family-wise correction ($p=0.0005$). Nearly all of it comes from the three degree scalars. Zeroing PINNACLE alone moves \textsc{systema} by $+0.000006$, far less even than its $9.2\%$ coverage would predict: nine percent of the degree scalars' effect would be of order $10^{-4}$, and this is fifty times smaller. Thin coverage is part of it; the rest is that the embedding carries almost nothing usable on the rows where it does resolve.

Three degree scalars carry a measured effect; the gated encoder (h1), which passes messages over the network they summarize, carries none we can detect. Whether the summary is worth \emph{more} is not settled: paired by seed, the graph-derived features' value minus h1 is $+0.0045$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.0012,+0.0101]$, $p{=}0.093$; a post hoc test), positive in all five seeds. We do not claim degree is a good predictor in absolute terms; $0.0036$ against a baseline of $0.0857$ is about four percent, and it clears correction largely because the no-graph arm is our lowest-variance one. The claim is about experimental design: the arm called ``no graph'' carries a measured graph effect, so a graph ablation should strip it too. 

\section{When a pre-registration freezes a mistake}
\label{app:prereg}

\paragraph{The rule, and what it cost.} Under the original rule, before the amendment this section is about, we built differential-expression matrices for six harmonized public candidates \citep{peidli2024scperturb}; the corrected rule later built ten (Table~\ref{tab:repl}). The pre-registration fixed the differential-expression unit as ``two or more replicate pseudobulks per (target, condition)'' at a $25$-cell floor, and specified that a dataset unable to meet it is dropped rather than switched to a different test. Four candidates fell out. The clause did what such clauses are for: when Replogle RPE1, the best-powered of those six candidates, yielded only $12$ of $2{,}393$ targets, we did not rescue it by splitting cells into random pseudo-replicates, which would have manufactured precision we did not have.

\paragraph{The audit, and why the rule was wrong.} The clause was also wrong, and it took an audit rather than a result to see it. The reference dataset's own supervised target is not built that way. It is an artifact published with the source screen, computed by its authors over all cells for each (perturbation, condition) against matched controls, with no per-replicate requirement. We had imposed on every candidate replication a standard we never imposed on the data the study rests on, and so discarded the best-powered replication then available for failing a test the reference itself would have failed. Under the reference's own standard, keeping the $25$-cell floor, the same file yields $2{,}122$ targets rather than $12$.

\paragraph{The failure mode this illustrates.} Discussion of pre-registration in machine learning is almost entirely about the failure mode it prevents: analytic flexibility exercised after seeing results. Pre-registration also has a failure mode of its own. It fixes the plan when the data are least understood, so a rule written then can be wrong in a way that stays invisible until something forces an audit. The commitment that gives pre-registration its value also makes such an error durable, because relaxing a pre-registered rule looks like the flexibility pre-registration exists to prevent. Our defense is procedural: the amendment is dated, it states that no replication arm had been trained when it was written, and it is outcome-symmetric, admitting datasets that could support a positive replication as readily as a null. Any pre-registration in this setting should include an explicit audit step that checks each rule against how the reference data were themselves constructed, before the rule is frozen.

\section{What the bound is, and what it is not}
\label{app:power}

\paragraph{What we could have detected, simulated rather than approximated.} We first reported a minimum detectable effect from a normal approximation on five paired per-seed differences, which assumed that the between-seed variance was the only variance. That assumption is no longer needed, because the variance is measured at three levels (Appendix~\ref{app:folds}), and the bar a result must clear here is not $p<0.05$ but a paired $t$ followed by Bonferroni and Holm over a family of four, which has no closed form. So we simulate it: each replicate draws the whole family, one contrast at the effect under test and three true nulls, runs the pipeline's own test and correction, and we repeat $2{,}000$ times per point. Table~\ref{tab:floor} collects the result. Two calibrations check that the simulation measures what we think. With no true effect it survives at $0.013$, the corrected rate, not the nominal $0.05$. And the reference screen's corrected-significant graph benefit, the untyped arm's $+0.0043$, needs $9$ seeds for $80\%$ power at its own measured spread of $0.0028$; we ran $7$, which the simulation puts at $70\%$ power, and it did survive both corrections.

Our headline null is an estimate of $-0.0009$, an order of magnitude inside the first of those. \paragraph{Graph arms are noisier across seeds.} The no-graph arm is stable across seeds and every graph arm is not. On the frozen fold the per-seed standard deviation of \textsc{systema} is $0.00051$ for \textsc{expression\_only} against $0.00545$ for \textsc{condition\_gated} ($10.8\times$), $0.00488$ for \textsc{typed\_static} ($9.6\times$) and $0.00301$ for \textsc{untyped\_gnn} ($5.9\times$); on the harder split, all at $n{=}5$, the ratios rise to $31.7\times$ for \textsc{condition\_gated}, $20.6\times$ for \textsc{untyped\_gnn} and $17.4\times$ for \textsc{typed\_static}, against a no-graph standard deviation of $0.00028$. For the gated arm the spread rises with no detectable change in the mean. Message passing over a mostly noisy neighborhood plausibly gives the optimizer a high-variance pathway to fit; whatever the cause, the added variance widens the interval a benefit must clear, which checklist item 10 generalizes.

\section{The detection floor, measured}
\label{app:floor}

The simulation in Appendix~\ref{app:power} says what this pipeline should be able to detect; this appendix measures what it does detect. We add a known graph-dependent component to the real responses at a ladder of sizes and report the smallest one recovered. For each perturbation target the component is the mean response of its protein-interaction neighbors, computed over \emph{training rows only}, scaled so its spread matches the training response's and multiplied by $\delta$, so $\delta$ reads as a fraction of a response standard deviation. A model that can read the graph can then predict a held-out target from its neighbors. Six conditions, two arms, four paired seeds each: $48$ training runs, no failures.

Two properties make the number meaningful, and both were fixed before any lane ran. No validation response reaches any injected value, including through the scaling constant, which a test asserts by perturbing a validation response and requiring the whole injection matrix to be bit-identical. And one rung is a \emph{negative control} in which each target receives some \emph{other} target's neighborhood at the largest $\delta$, preserving the injected component's size and distribution while destroying its relation to the graph. Rows outside the training and validation folds are left bit-identical, so the sequestered split is untouched.

\paragraph{What the injection bought, over what was already there.} Each rung is tested against zero, and this fold already carries a real graph benefit of $+0.0048$ with no injection at all, so a rung can clear on the pre-existing benefit alone. Subtracting the same seed's un-injected gap leaves the increment column of Table~\ref{tab:floor2}, a weaker claim than the primary: only $\delta{=}0.40$ has an increment whose own interval excludes zero. So this pipeline detects graph-dependent structure at $0.02$, but at four seeds it cannot separate an \emph{added} benefit from the one already present until $\delta{=}0.40$. That subtraction is post hoc, and it was written and committed while two rungs were still training.

\paragraph{Do not compare rungs by their own intervals.} The $\Delta$ column's intervals are within-rung and paired on the seed, so they are tight, and the $\delta{=}0.10$ rung's $+0.0041$ sits outside its neighbors' intervals on both sides. That comparison is not licensed: each rung is a different dataset, and the quantity with the right error bars for comparing across rungs is the increment, whose intervals are two to four times wider and overlap at every rung.

\paragraph{The simulation and the ladder measure different instruments.} Appendix~\ref{app:power} put the smallest detectable effect at $0.0075$ \textsc{systema} on this fold at seven seeds, and the $\delta{=}0.02$ rung produced a gap of $+0.0088$ detected at four. The two are close, but the simulation runs at the frozen fold's between-seed spread of $0.0043$, while the ladder pairs on identical injected data and carries $0.00106$ at that rung, four times less. The built instrument beat the modeled one because it is quieter.

\paragraph{The scrambled control carries a finding of its own.} Its increment over the zero point is $-0.0050$ ($[-0.0083,-0.0016]$, $p=0.02$): injecting graph-shaped structure that follows the \emph{wrong} graph destroys the real benefit that was there. That separates three readings of a null (the prior is uninformative, the encoder discards it, or the graph is wrong): only the last removes a benefit the correct graph provides.

\paragraph{The floor of the arm the null is about.} The ladder above uses \textsc{untyped\_gnn}, chosen in advance on cost and sensitivity, so it bounds the \emph{pipeline} rather than the gated encoder. Re-running the identical six rungs with \textsc{condition\_gated} against \textsc{expression\_only}, on the same frozen fold, the same four seeds and the same injected matrices, with $\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}{=}0$ so the gate stays live, recovers \emph{nothing at any injected size} (Table~\ref{tab:floorgated}).

\paragraph{Variance explains the small rungs, not the largest.} The gated arm's per-seed paired standard deviation is $1.7$ to $9.5\times$ the untyped arm's across the five injected rungs, largest at the small ones and consistent with the $10.8\times$ per-arm seed spread of Appendix~\ref{app:power}; on the scrambled control the two arms are equally noisy ($0.96\times$). That noise buries the small rungs. It does not account for the largest one. At $\delta{=}0.40$ the gated arm carries only $1.7\times$ the untyped arm's spread while its estimate is $4.1\times$ smaller, and handing it the untyped arm's standard deviation outright still leaves it short ($t{=}2.00$, $p{=}0.14$, $0.84$ corrected over six). At that rung the shortfall is the mean, not the noise. Matching the untyped arm's standard error at four seeds would take $11$ seeds at $\delta{=}0.40$ and $63$ to $358$ at the other four.

\paragraph{On the increment, neither arm clears correction.} The untyped arm enters this ladder already $+0.0048$ ahead of no graph with no injection, and the gated arm enters at $-0.0009$. Subtracting each arm's own zero point leaves what the injection bought, and there no increment excludes zero until the largest rung: $+0.0135$ ($p{=}0.032$) for the untyped arm and $+0.0053$ ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0007,+0.0099]$, $p{=}0.035$) for the gated one, both uncorrected, neither surviving correction over six. The untyped increment is $2.5\times$ the gated one, and that ratio is the whole of the measured difference in injected-signal sensitivity. ``Clears at $0.02$ against nothing up to $0.40$'' is a statement about levels and should be read as one.

The claim this supports is narrower than blindness to graph structure: \emph{at four paired seeds}, the gated encoder's advantage over no graph clears correction at no injected size up to $0.40$ response SDs, twenty times the size at which the untyped arm's does. Our headline null is measured with that instrument, so it describes what this encoder can resolve at this budget, not whether a PPI prior helps.

\paragraph{Two limits, both fixed before the ladder ran.} First, the untyped ladder measures the \emph{best} arm's floor. Amendment 6.5 registered \textsc{untyped\_gnn} against \textsc{expression\_only} rather than the gated arm, on cost ($2.3$ to $2.5$ GPU-hours per lane against $7.5$ to $16$) and on sensitivity: when the ladder was registered, the untyped arm was the only one with a corrected-significant positive, so a floor measured with it bounds the best detector we had, and weaker arms need at least as much signal. It says the pipeline can carry a graph effect of $0.02$ response SDs to a detectable contrast; it does \emph{not} say the typed encoder can.

Second, the injected component is the mean response of a target's PPI neighbors, which is, up to scaling, the quantity a message-passing layer computes. The ladder therefore measures sensitivity to a signal shaped like the architecture's own inductive bias, the most favorable case there is. The scrambled control rules out the arms responding to injected magnitude regardless of the graph; it does not rule out that the injected signal has the form this architecture is built to find. A real graph effect in biology need not be a neighbor mean, and a floor measured against one does not transfer to one that is not.

\paragraph{The scrambled damage is measured at one magnitude.} We measured it at a single injection size, $0.40$ response SDs, which is large. Whether it scales down is not settled here, and a second control rung at $\delta{=}0.10$ would not settle it: proportional damage would be $-0.0012$ there, against a minimum detectable effect at four seeds of $0.0044$ on this control's own seed spread, $0.0082$ on the injected rungs' median, and $0.0080$ once the enlarged ladder family is corrected for. Separating proportional from absent damage needs $25$ seeds on the control's spread and $81$ on the rungs', against the four the ladder ran. We report the claim at the magnitude we measured and leave the scaling open; an underpowered rung would have produced a null that meant nothing, the mistake our architecture search already made (Appendix~\ref{app:archsearch}).

\section{The null under other people's endpoints}
\label{app:metrics}

Outside results differ from ours in splits and metrics at once. The metric half can be checked without retraining anything, because every arm's per-row predictions for the frozen validation fold are persisted. We re-scored all four arms at all five seeds under five endpoints, each verified against the paper that reports it: Pearson-$\Delta$ over all genes, which TxPert reports; Pearson correlation and mean squared error over each perturbation's top-$20$ differentially expressed genes, which \textsc{gears} reports \citep{roohani2024gears}; the E-distance as scPerturb computes it \citep{peidli2024scperturb}; and the energy distance with plain Euclidean distances. The differentially expressed subset is taken from the \emph{observed} response, never the prediction.

Two cautions apply to the distance endpoints. First, scPerturb computes its E-distance from squared Euclidean distances, and in that form the statistic collapses algebraically to $2\lVert \bar{X}-\bar{Y}\rVert^2$: a difference of means, blind to a difference in spread. We report it for commensurability and treat the plain-Euclidean energy distance as the distributional evidence. Second, this pipeline predicts one pseudobulk response per (target, condition), so both distances compare the distribution of \emph{responses across held-out perturbations}, never cell populations; claims about single-cell distributions stay out of reach.

Five endpoints times four contrasts is twenty simultaneous tests, and correcting only within each endpoint and then reporting whichever endpoint was kind is the look-elsewhere error. We fixed the correction in the pre-registration before reading the fold: both bars are computed, and a survival claim requires the across-metric one, Bonferroni and Holm together. Table~\ref{tab:metrics} reports all twenty cells.

Both effects are real and both clear correction. The untyped graph makes the broad transcriptional response more accurate and the genes each perturbation moves hardest \emph{less} accurate, and the cumulative crossover sits between the $250$th and $500$th DE gene. Which of the two a paper reports is a choice of $k$, and $k$ is a convention: $20$ because that is what \textsc{gears} reports, all genes because that is what TxPert reports. We cannot say which a reader should prefer. Our own null is stated on \textsc{systema}, one endpoint among several that do not agree, and readers should hold it to the same standard. The typed contrast h2a is the one row here that does not depend on the choice: it is negative and corrected-significant at every $k$ we tried. With live gates the gated arm traces the untyped arm's shape, with as large a deficit on the most-moved genes and a smaller gain elsewhere: corrected-significant harm at $k\leq50$, a sign change between $1{,}000$ and $2{,}500$, and no corrected benefit at any $k$.

\paragraph{The crossover is a property of the cumulative statistic.} Every top-$k$ set contains all smaller ones, so a sign change in Table~\ref{tab:ksweep} only bounds where the per-gene effect turned. Re-scoring the same stored predictions on \emph{disjoint} rank intervals, the increments between consecutive rows of that table, separates the two (Table~\ref{tab:bins}). The untyped arm's significant deficit is confined to each perturbation's twenty most-moved genes, where it is large ($-0.0424$), negative in $5/5$ seeds, and clears both corrections over all $36$ cells. By rank $101$ the per-interval contrast is already positive in $5/5$ seeds, and from rank $251$ down to the $10{,}282$nd gene every interval is positive and clears both corrections. The cumulative crossover sits at $250$ to $500$ only because each running average carries the top-$20$ deficit forward until enough positive mass accumulates to cancel it; the per-gene effect turns between rank $100$ and $250$. Table~\ref{tab:ksweep} therefore says that the untyped graph helps everywhere except on the handful of genes each perturbation moves hardest, not that it helps beyond the $500$th gene. That is the one place in this paper where h1 clears correction in the graph's favor. It is a pre-registered secondary slicing, the whole-profile h1 stays null on every endpoint, and the top-$20$ interval points the other way, so we report it beside the null rather than in place of it.

\section{Reproducibility detail}
\label{app:repro}

Every number in \S\ref{sec:null} is read from persisted artifacts rather than from logs: the five-seed contrast family and both corrections from the multi-seed robustness report, and the second-metric replication from the second-metric report. Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} is drawn from the twenty-two-epoch sweep artifact; its row counts come from that sweep's run logs. The gate-health precondition is a separate probe that prints the collapse factor against initialization; we read it before any downstream analysis, because at $\sim$$10^{-7}$ every edge deletion is a floating-point no-op and every rationale contrast returns undecidable by construction.

\paragraph{Compute and schedules.} The $379$ training lanes ran on A100 80GB cards ($0.4$ to $12$ GPU-hours each in the headline family), each up to $20$ epochs at batch size $8$ with early stopping on validation loss; Figure~\ref{fig:lambda}'s sweep instead runs a fixed $22$ epochs at batch size $4$, and the architecture search $5$ epochs.
