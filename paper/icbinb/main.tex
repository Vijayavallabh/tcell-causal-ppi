\begin{abstract}

Does a typed protein-interaction prior improve prediction of how primary human T cells respond to
genetic perturbation? We report two failures, and the more useful one is not the one we went looking
for. The first is an instrument failure that nearly published itself: a textbook edge-sparsity
regularizer, written as an unnormalized sum over the edges of each sampled neighborhood, is roughly
$103\times$ the response term at its default weight and drives every learnable edge gate to
$\sim$$10^{-7}$ inside the first epoch, in all five seeds. Training completes and reports a plausible
score, so a graph-versus-no-graph comparison silently becomes no-graph versus no-graph. The second is
the null itself. With the gates demonstrably live, the evidence-gated graph is indistinguishable from
an expression-only baseline ($\Delta\,\textsc{systema}=-0.0009$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0072,+0.0054]$,
$n{=}5$, family-wise corrected), and the typed null holds across seven further Perturb-seq screens.
The one contrast we were entitled to call significant then failed its own replication on a harder
split. Of five candidate causes we adjudicate, the two that carry the argument are not about biology.
Our baseline was never graph-free: zeroing its graph-derived features costs $-0.0036$
\textsc{systema} under correction, more than the message passing over eight million typed edges we
set out to measure. And an arm we had never run overturned the reading: strip the typing and gating,
keep the raw topology, and that plain arm beats the baseline on our own screen ($+0.0043$, $n{=}7$,
surviving Bonferroni and Holm) and on Replogle RPE1 ($+0.0675$), and \emph{loses} to it on Norman
($-0.0790$); three of eight datasets clear correction, in \emph{disagreeing} directions. So the prior
is not what fails but the encoder we built to exploit it, and a ``graph versus no graph'' null can be
an artifact of the encoder rather than evidence about the graph. The null is also instrument-bound:
the gated arm it rests on clears at no injected signal up to $0.40$ response SDs, where our best
graph arm clears at $0.02$. We close with the checklist we wish we had used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Genome-scale Perturb-seq now measures the transcriptional consequence of knocking down essentially
every expressed gene in primary human cells
\citep{dixit2016perturbseq,replogle2022genomescale,zhu2025gwperturbseq}, and protein-interaction
networks encode decades of curated mechanism. Does feeding the network to a model help it predict the
response to a target it has never seen?

The literature does not agree. Careful benchmarks report that deep and foundation models fail to beat
deliberately simple linear or mean baselines \citep{ahlmanneltze2025linear,wongmoccia2025crispr}, and
that tabular foundation models match specialized architectures \citep{hollmann2025tabpfn,royer2026tfm}; other work
reports that graph and foundation models do win, under their own splits and metrics
\citep{wenkel2026txpert,cole2026foundation}. The disagreement turns on split design and metric choice
more than on architecture.

We built a model designed to give the graph its best chance, and then spent most of our effort
discovering that our own measuring instrument was broken. This paper is organized around that
experience rather than around the architecture.

\paragraph{What we report.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{An instrument failure that reads as a result} (\S\ref{sec:confound}). A standard
structural penalty annihilates the mechanism the study exists to test, inside the first epoch, in
every seed, while the experiment keeps running and reporting numbers. We give the mechanism, the
empirical signature, and the one-line fix.
\item \textbf{A well-controlled null, and a claim of ours that did not replicate} (\S\ref{sec:null}).
With live gates, paired seeds, a biologically blocked target-out-of-distribution split and family-wise
error control, our \emph{typed, gated} graph does not help on this screen, and the typed static
graph's null holds on seven more. The parity survives a second metric and a harder split; our one
corrected-significant contrast does not.
\item \textbf{A decomposition of \emph{why}} (\S\ref{sec:causes}). Five candidate causes, each with
the evidence that speaks to it and an explicit verdict, and one of them, our own architecture,
overturned by an arm we had not thought to run.
\item \textbf{A checklist} (\S\ref{sec:checklist}) for anyone running a gated- or masked-structure
ablation.
\end{itemize}

The null itself is uninteresting; nulls in a near-null regime usually are. Three things around it are
not, and none is specific to graphs or to biology. A conventional regularizer can switch off the
mechanism under test and leave the run looking healthy. The ablation the field calls ``no graph'' is
not graph-free, and on our fold what it discards outweighs what it measures. And a null attributed to
a prior can belong instead to the encoder built to exploit it, which one extra arm would have caught
at any point in the project.

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Task and data.} For each (perturbation, condition) pair we predict the transcriptional
response to knocking down a target gene, in a latent program space. The data is the genome-scale
primary human CD4$^{+}$ T-cell CRISPRi Perturb-seq resource of \citet{zhu2025gwperturbseq} (four
donors; conditions Rest, Stim8hr, Stim48hr). The supervised target is a differential-expression matrix
of $33{,}983$ (perturbation $\times$ condition) rows over $10{,}282$ genes, covering $11{,}526$
distinct gene targets, modeled in a $K{=}128$ sparse-PCA program basis \citep{zou2006sparsepca} fit
\emph{inside} each training fold.

\paragraph{Metric and split.} The primary endpoint is \textsc{systema}, the perturbation-specific
delta-correlation of \citet{vinas2025systema}, which asks whether a prediction recovers the
\emph{correct} perturbation's response profile rather than a generic average treatment effect. The
headline split is biologically blocked target-OOD: targets are grouped by centered ESM-2 sequence
similarity (cosine $\geq 0.85$, group size capped at $5\%$) so that no training target is a close
paralog of a validation target, and every response-derived transform is fit inside the training fold.
Five paired seeds share one frozen fold. Four contrasts are pre-registered and controlled with
\emph{both} Bonferroni and Holm \citep{holm1979}; a contrast counts only if it clears both. A benefit
counts only if it is positive and clears both corrections at $n\geq4$ paired seeds, a bar fixed before
any lane ran. Everything below rests on $379$ training lanes on A100 80GB cards, $2.3$ to $16.8$
GPU-hours each, $20$ epochs at batch size $8$ ($22$ in Figure~\ref{fig:lambda}, $5$ in the architecture search).

\section{Proposed approach}
\label{sec:approach}

\paragraph{Graph.} Nodes are proteins and protein complexes over $\sim$$8$M typed edges: physical PPI
\citep{huttlin2021bioplex,luck2020huri,oughtred2021biogrid}, co-complex \citep{tsitsiridis2023corum},
and functional association \citep{szklarczyk2023string}, each carrying a confidence score (counts in
Appendix~\ref{app:arch}). Functional associations are $85\%$ of the edge budget, which is exactly the
tier a skeptic would suspect of adding noise, and \S\ref{sec:causes} returns to that suspicion.

\paragraph{The nested family.} Four arms differ only in how the graph enters, so every contrast is an
ablation inside one architecture rather than a contest between codebases: \textsc{expression\_only}
(graph pathway off), \textsc{typed\_static} (typed encoder, condition gate pinned to $1$),
\textsc{condition\_gated} (the full model, learnable per-edge gates conditioned on culture context),
and \textsc{untyped\_gnn} (a homogeneous GCN \citep{kipf2017gcn} diagnostic). Architecture detail is
in Appendix~\ref{app:arch}.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} The nested family gives three pre-registered contrasts, each isolating one
step. \textbf{h1} is \textsc{condition\_gated} $-$ \textsc{expression\_only}: does the full model beat
no graph? \textbf{h2a} is \textsc{typed\_static} $-$ \textsc{expression\_only}: does the typed graph
beat no graph without the gate? \textbf{h2b} is \textsc{condition\_gated} $-$ \textsc{typed\_static}:
does conditioning the gate on culture context add anything? A fourth, the promotion margin, is
\textsc{untyped\_gnn} $-$ \textsc{expression\_only}: does \emph{any} graph beat no graph? h1 is the
hypothesis the model was built for and the one the headline null is about. The approach works only if
curated interaction evidence carries co-regulation information that expression alone does not, and if
the gate can suppress the edges that do not. Both preconditions are testable, and
\S\ref{sec:confound} shows the second failing silently.

\section{Observed outcome}
\label{sec:outcome}

\subsection{The instrument was off}
\label{sec:confound}

Our first multi-seed comparison found the graph inert: edge gates sat near $10^{-7}$ and the graph and
no-graph arms agreed to within spare parameters. We recorded a clean negative result. A later
gate-health probe showed the graph had been switched off before it learned anything.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} The training objective carries an edge-sparsity and low-confidence penalty
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{graph}}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{r,b,e}\big(|\alpha^{r}_{b,e}| + (1-\mathrm{conf}^{r}_{b,e})(\alpha^{r}_{b,e})^2\big),
\label{eq:graphloss}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_e$ is the per-edge gate. The inner sum runs over \emph{every edge of each sampled
neighborhood}, tens of thousands of them, and is divided only by the batch size $n$, never by the edge
count. At the default weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}{=}10^{-2}$ the term is $\approx$$103\times$ the
mean-reduced response term. AdamW \citep{kingma2015adam,loshchilov2019adamw} rescales each parameter's
step by its own gradient's running magnitude, so a penalty of that relative size does not merely
shrink the gates; it plausibly fixes the \emph{direction} of their updates. Sparsity pressure that
should have been a mild prior becomes the objective.

\paragraph{Signature.} Every gate collapses to $\sim$$10^{-7}$ within the first epoch, in all five
seeds, and a monotone sweep in $\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}$ tracks the collapse
(Figure~\ref{fig:lambda}); a penalty of only $10^{-5}$ already puts the mean gate below $10^{-3}$ with
nearly every gate dead. The run does not crash, warn, or diverge. It trains to completion and reports
a plausible \textsc{systema}.

\paragraph{Why it is dangerous.} The comparison this pipeline exists to make is graph versus no-graph.
With the gates annihilated the graph arm \emph{is} the no-graph arm, so the contrast compares a model
to itself and returns a small difference with a tight interval: an identity check reported in the
format of evidence. Worse, it fails silently in the direction we were prepared to believe. We expected
a null, and the broken instrument produced one.

\paragraph{Fix and precondition.} The fix is one line: normalize the penalty per edge, or equivalently
set $\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}{=}0$, since the confidence weighting and the top-$k$ rationale already
bound reliance on the graph. That swings the mean final gate by $4.5\times10^{6}$, from $\sim$$10^{-7}$
to a live $0.57$ to $0.77$. We now treat the gate mean as a \emph{precondition, not an output}: below
a dead-gate threshold ($10^{-3}$) the graph comparison is undecidable by construction, and the run is
rejected rather than reported. Reading it costs three minutes and decides whether a multi-hour run
means anything.

\subsection{With the instrument working, the graph still does not help}
\label{sec:null}

Table~\ref{tab:family} reports the corrected five-seed comparison on live gates. The evidence-gated graph is statistically indistinguishable from expression-only ($\Delta\,\textsc{systema}=-0.0009$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0072,+0.0054]$, $p=0.71$); the interval crosses zero and the contrast clears neither correction. The typed \emph{static} graph is worse than no-graph on this fold ($-0.0131$, $p=0.0036$, surviving both corrections): forcing every typed edge on, without the gate's ability to switch noisy edges off, hurts. \paragraph{The null survives a second metric, a harder split and a third fold.} Re-scored under
program-space Pearson-delta, which does not subtract the training mean, both conclusions hold: parity
for the gated graph ($-0.0005$, $p=0.64$) and a deficit for the static graph ($-0.0067$), with program
cosine agreeing ($-0.0005$, $p=0.65$). Pearson-delta carries under half the paired standard deviation
of \textsc{systema}, so it has \emph{more} power to find a benefit, and finds none. Re-drawing the blocked
split at a stricter threshold, and running a third fold at an intermediate one, return the same
corrected verdict. The point estimates differ across folds ($-0.0009$, $+0.0082$, $+0.0005$), which is
why we report a per-fold bound rather than a general one (Appendix~\ref{app:power}).

\paragraph{Our one significant contrast did not replicate.} The harder split is also a replication
test of h2a, and h2a fails it. On the frozen fold the static graph is worse by $-0.0131$ and survives
both corrections; on the harder split, at the same $n{=}5$, the deficit is $-0.0012$, $95\%$ CI
$[-0.0074,+0.0050]$, $p=0.61$, clearing neither. That is an elevenfold shrink. The per-seed
differences show why: $-0.0010$, $-0.0013$, $-0.0033$, $-0.0070$, $+0.0066$. One seed reverses sign,
and the seed dominating h2a is the seed dominating h1, so the instability is seed-level rather than
arm-level. h2b and the untyped-GCN margin move the same way
($+0.0122\!\to\!+0.0017$, $+0.0045\!\to\!+0.0028$); on the harder split \emph{nothing} in the family
survives correction.

The third fold shows the failure is not one thing but two. On the intermediate fold h2a is $-0.0122$,
within $7\%$ of the frozen $-0.0131$, yet clears no correction ($p=0.23$) because the across-seed
standard deviation is $0.0169$ there against $0.0049$ on the frozen fold; on the hardest fold the
estimate itself collapses to $-0.0012$ ($p=0.61$). One replication failed because the noise grew, the other
because the effect shrank, and only the second says anything about the effect. This is the most
uncomfortable result in the paper. The parity claim holds across folds, metrics and split difficulty;
the one \emph{positive} claim we were entitled to make under multiplicity control does not, so
``typed static graphs hurt'' should be read as a statement about one split.

\subsection{The null replicates, but the graph is not the thing that fails}
\label{sec:repl}

We replicated the comparison on seven further Perturb-seq screens \citep{peidli2024scperturb}:
Frangieh melanoma, three Replogle screens including a genome-wide one ($9{,}730$ targets), Tian
iPSC-neuron CRISPRi, Norman K562 and Tian CRISPRa. That spans four cell types beyond the reference's
own, both perturbation directions, and a hundredfold range of target counts. Each dataset gets its own blocked target-OOD split, its own program basis refit inside its own
training fold, four seeds per arm, and the family correction at the size that dataset can support
(Appendix~\ref{app:repl}).

\paragraph{The typed null holds, and is now bounded rather than merely unrefuted.} Pooled over the
seven replication datasets the typed static graph is worth $+0.0018$ \textsc{systema} (fixed-effect
$95\%$ CI $[-0.0005,+0.0040]$; random-effects $+0.0018$, $[-0.0028,+0.0065]$; $I^2{=}39.2\%$, $Q$
$p{=}0.13$). The absence of significant heterogeneity matters as much as the estimate: four cell
types, both perturbation directions and that hundredfold spread of target counts ($100$ to $9{,}730$)
return the same answer, and the genome-wide screen, where power is highest, is the tightest single
point of all ($+0.0008$). Frangieh, the one dataset where the condition gate has anything to gate, is
underpowered rather than null (Appendix~\ref{app:repl}).

\paragraph{The result that breaks the pattern points at us, not at the prior.} On Replogle RPE1 the
\textsc{untyped\_gnn} arm (the same PPI topology with edge typing and condition gating removed) beats
the expression-only baseline by $+0.0675$ \textsc{systema} ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0316,+0.1033]$; per-seed
$+0.036,+0.072,+0.089,+0.073$; $p{=}0.009$, Bonferroni and Holm both $0.019$ at family size two). It
is the only contrast anywhere in this project that survives family-wise correction in the direction of
the graph helping. On that same dataset the \emph{typed} arm goes the other way ($-0.0196$). Where the
graph does help, our typing-and-gating machinery is what discards the benefit.

its frozen fold the untyped arm is $+0.0043$ \textsc{systema} at $n{=}7$ ($95\%$ CI

\section{The checklist we wish we had used}
\label{sec:checklist}

\vspace{2pt}\hrule\vspace{4pt}
{\small
\textbf{Before you believe a structure ablation:}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt \parskip0pt
\item \textbf{Log the mechanism's own health every epoch}, not just the loss and the metric. For a
gated graph that is the mean gate; for a masked module, the mask occupancy. A mechanism can be dead
while the run is green.
\item \textbf{Declare a collapse threshold in advance and treat breaching it as \emph{undecidable},
not as a result.} A disabled mechanism trivially ties the baseline, and that tie will read as your
hypothesis.
\item \textbf{Compare every regularization term to the task loss in magnitude, not in hyperparameter
value.} A weight of $10^{-2}$ sounds small; ours was $103\times$ the response term because the sum was
unnormalized. Print the ratio.
\item \textbf{Normalize structural penalties by the structure's size.} Sums over sampled neighborhoods
scale with the sample, so the effective penalty changes with the sampler.
\item \textbf{Check that your headline survives a large change in the offending hyperparameter.} Ours
moved the gates by $4.5\times10^{6}$ and the answer not at all, which is what let us keep the null.
\item \textbf{Ask what your baseline actually contains, then ablate it.} Ours received three PPI
degree scalars; zeroing them cost more than the message passing we were testing. Six GPU-hours settled
a cause we had written down as future work.
\item \textbf{Audit feature \emph{coverage}, not the feature list.} Our $128$-d contextual embedding
is a zero vector for $90.8\%$ of rows, because its cell-type context covers a fraction of the targets.
A feature present in the schema and absent in the data quietly changes what your ablation means, in
either direction.
\item \textbf{Run a structure-only arm beside your clever one.} Strip the typing, gating and
attention, and keep the raw topology. Across eight datasets that plain arm clears correction three
times \emph{in disagreeing directions} ($+0.0043$, $+0.0675$, $-0.0790$): raw structure does not
reliably help either, and the point is that the typed arm alone cannot tell you which. An architecture
search does not substitute. Ours tried $14$ cells and every one varied \emph{how} the typed encoder
passes messages, never whether the typing belonged.
\item \textbf{Redraw your split before you interpret a split sweep.} Regenerate one setting at a
different partition seed and compare the spread to the range your knob spans. Ours moved the
difficulty statistic $0.103$ against a designed range of $0.056$, so the sweep was measuring the seed
more than the knob.
\item \textbf{Report per-arm variance across seeds, not only the paired contrast.} Our graph arms
carry $6$ to $32\times$ the seed-to-seed spread of the no-graph arm at the same mean. A component can
change the spread without changing the expectation, and the paired contrast will not show you that.
\item \textbf{Pre-register the contrast family and correct for it.} With four simultaneous contrasts,
two of ours reach nominal significance and neither survives both corrections.
\item \textbf{Inject a signal you know is there, with a scrambled control.} A null is a claim about
your instrument. Ours clears correction at an injected signal of $0.02$ response SDs and clears nothing
% AUTO 2026-08-26: this cited our instrument as a clean pass while the arm our null is about fails
% the same test. A checklist item that overstates its own worked example is worse than no example.
when the same component follows a \emph{scrambled} graph (Appendix~\ref{app:floor}). The scrambled
rung is the one people skip, and it separates reading structure from responding to magnitude. Run it
on the arm your claim is about: ours clears on the untyped arm and never clears on the gated one,
at twenty times the size.
\item \textbf{Slice the metric \emph{disjointly}, and check the resolution.} Cumulative top-$k$ scores
contain every smaller $k$, so a sign change only bounds where the effect turned. Ours hides a
$-0.0424$ deficit on twenty genes inside a positive decile of $1{,}028$.
\item \textbf{Replicate the contrast that \emph{survived}, not only the one that failed.} Effort goes
into hardening a null. Our one corrected-significant result was the one that did not reproduce on a
harder split, and we would not have known had we treated that split as a robustness check on the null
alone.
\end{enumerate}
}\vspace{2pt}\hrule\vspace{4pt}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{A bounded null, and the bound is per fold, not general.} No fold yields a benefit surviving
correction, and no single fold's upper limit bounds the effect elsewhere (Appendix~\ref{app:power}).
% AUTO 2026-08-26: "the typed arm's own floor is unmeasured" was true when written and is now false.
The instrument is not the limit for our best graph arm, but at $n{=}4$ it is for the gated one, which
clears at no injected size up to $0.40$, the largest rung we ran (Appendix~\ref{app:floor}).

\textbf{Our own pre-registration nearly prevented the replication.} Under the rules as written, four
of six candidate datasets fell out. Auditing that decision produced the more useful finding, and it is
about pre-registration rather than about this dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). The replication has
since run under the amended rule (\S\ref{sec:repl}), so the null is no longer one screen, but two
narrower limits replace it: h1 still rests on a single replication, and no dataset replicates the
reference \emph{cell type} (Appendix~\ref{app:repl}).

\textbf{Scope.} Gains reported elsewhere \citep{wenkel2026txpert,cole2026foundation} use different
splits and metrics, so our bound constrains this task rather than adjudicating those claims. Re-scored
under their endpoints our null holds, but the untyped contrast \emph{reverses sign} between TxPert's
and \textsc{gears}' headline metrics (Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}).

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to test whether a protein-interaction prior helps predict T-cell perturbation responses.
The harder problem throughout was trusting our own measurement. A conventional regularizer switched
off the mechanism under test while the pipeline kept reporting plausible numbers, in the direction we
already expected. Repaired, the typed gated graph gives a bounded null that holds across seven further
screens. Our one contrast that cleared multiplicity control then failed its own replication, and the
fold statistic we would have used to explain that failure moved as much under a reseed as across the
range we varied.

% AUTO 2026-08-26: this replayed the abstract almost verbatim. The finding is stated there and in
% full, with numbers, in Section~\ref{sec:repl}; the conclusion's job is the consequence.
Then one untried arm overturned that reading: untyped, ungated topology clears correction on three
datasets, in \emph{disagreeing} directions. The null we measured was never a fact about the
prior. It was a fact about our encoder, which flattens a signal
whose sign we cannot predict from cell type, perturbation direction, or anything else we can see. Our
$14$-cell architecture search had refuted the architecture as a cause, but every cell varied \emph{how}
the typed encoder passes messages and none asked whether the typing belonged.

Two things generalize. The baseline everyone calls ``no graph'' already carries graph-derived
features, so that ablation does not measure what its name says, in anyone's hands. And a negative
result about a prior is only as good as the simplest version of that prior you bothered to run. Ours
cost one arm. Without it we would have published the wrong cause, and with five of our eight datasets
the wrong sign.

\section{Model architecture}
\label{app:arch}

EG-IPG has four modules: a perturbation and context encoder, a typed evidence-gated graph encoder, a program decoder with a graph/expression mixture gate, and a sparse rationale head, in the GNNExplainer lineage \citep{ying2019gnnexplainer}, fitted after the predictor is frozen (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).

\paragraph{Perturbation and context encoder.} The target protein is represented by frozen features only, with \emph{no} trainable gene-ID embedding, so the model cannot memorize target identity and must generalize to unseen genes: ESM-2 650M ($1280$-d) \citep{lin2023esm2}, PINNACLE ($128$-d) \citep{li2024pinnacle}, three PPI degrees, and control-derived baseline expression, $1412$-d in total. This is fused with a condition embedding and donor context into an intervention vector $h_{\mathrm{do}}\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$. Note that this vector, and therefore the \textsc{expression\_only} arm, contains graph-derived features; see \S\ref{sec:causes}D. Measured coverage over the $33{,}983$ DE rows: ESM-2 resolves for $99.4\%$, physical and functional PPI degree are nonzero for $96.5\%$ and $98.4\%$, complex degree for $29.5\%$, and PINNACLE for $9.2\%$ (the embedding store returns a zero vector on a miss).

\paragraph{Typed evidence-gated graph encoder.} Around each target we sample a $2$-hop neighborhood capped at $512$ nodes and run $L{=}3$ relational message-passing layers \citep{schlichtkrull2018rgcn}. For an edge $u\!\to\!v$ of relation $r$,
\begin{equation}
m_{u\to v}^{(l),r}=\tanh\!\big(W^{(l),r}_{\mathrm{sign}}h_u^{(l-1)}\big)\odot\mathrm{ReLU}\!\big(W^{(l),r}_{\mathrm{mag}}h_u^{(l-1)}\big),
\end{equation}
a signed message whose $\tanh$ factor encodes activation or inhibition and whose $\mathrm{ReLU}$ factor encodes magnitude. The condition gate
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{u\to v}^{r}(c)=\sigma\!\big(w_{\mathrm{gate}}^{r}\cdot[\,h_{\mathrm{cond}}(c)\,\|\,f_e\,]+b_{\mathrm{gate}}^{r}\big)\in[0,1]
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
is applied before aggregation, so the same physical edge can be active in one context and silent in another. Unlike graph attention, this is an independent per-edge sigmoid conditioned on culture context and the edge's provenance features, not a query-key similarity normalized over a neighborhood. Its closest precedent is differentiable edge masking \citep{schlichtkrull2021graphmask}, which also learns a per-edge gate under a sparsity penalty; the confound of \S\ref{sec:confound} is a hazard of that construction generally and not of this model alone. Nodes update residually and a $4$-head cross-attention readout queried by $h_{\mathrm{do}}$ pools node states into $h_{\mathrm{graph}}\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$.

\paragraph{Decoder and mixture gate.} The decoder predicts a program delta from the graph and a parallel expression-only prediction that bypasses it, then mixes them with a learned $\gamma$; setting the graph encoder to null forces $\gamma{=}0$ and recovers the \textsc{expression\_only} member exactly. This is what makes the family a nested ablation rather than a comparison of two code paths.

\section{Which part of the typing does the damage?}
\label{app:mechanism}

\S\ref{sec:causes} localizes the deficit to edge typing: $-0.0120$ \textsc{systema} at $n{=}7$, seven of
seven seeds, surviving both corrections. It does not say what about typing costs that. Two explanations
are confounded inside the number. The relation partition may be the wrong inductive bias; or typed
message passing simply carries four times the message parameters over the same edges, in which case the
damage is capacity and has nothing to do with evidence types. Two arms separate them (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}), both registered
with their decision rule before either was trained.

\textsc{typed\_shared} ties one message module across all four relations instead of one each, cutting
$2{,}396{,}160$ message parameters to $599{,}040$ and the model's total parameters by $34\%$ ($5{,}254{,}884$ to $3{,}457{,}764$), and changing nothing
else. \textsc{typed\_permuted} keeps the per-relation modules and reassigns every protein-protein edge's
relation label at random, preserving each relation's edge count exactly. The relabelling happens
\emph{after} neighborhood sampling, because the sampler ranks candidate neighbors by relation, so
permuting the stored graph would have changed which neighbors enter the subgraph rather than only which
weights process them.

What remains is what all three typed arms share and the untyped one does not. Both diagnostic arms sit
well below the untyped \textsc{gcn} ($-0.0172$ and $-0.0111$) and below the no-graph baseline ($-0.0126$

under study was not resolving these arms at all, so ``refuted within the searched space'' was never

relation's contribution scales with its degree, so \textsc{functional\_assoc} ($85\%$ of all edges,

residual needs about $21$ seeds, half of it about $77$. Three more arms at five seeds would have been
underpowered by construction. Stopping also preserves the $p$ above; the reason is the power
calculation, not the $p$.

\section{Candidate causes A, B and E in full}
\label{app:causes}

\paragraph{A. The task is close to null for everyone.} Centroid accuracy sits at floor ($\approx$$0.001$) for every arm, and all four trained arms occupy a band from $0.072$ to $0.090$. An independent tabular benchmark reports this exact screen as a near-null-signal regime in which models barely separate from the perturbed mean \citep{royer2026tfm}. If there is little perturbation-specific structure to recover, no prior can route it. This does not excuse the graph, but it bounds how much any component could have contributed, and it is the reason we report a \emph{bounded} null rather than a claim of zero.

\paragraph{B. The metric is not hiding a benefit.} \textsc{systema} is deliberately conservative about average-treatment-effect shortcuts, so it is fair to ask whether it also suppresses a real gain. The same predictions re-scored under Pearson-delta, an endpoint with less than half the paired standard deviation and therefore more power, give the same answer. Refuted.

\paragraph{E. The usable signal sits in the tier we would have pruned.} Functional associations are $85\%$ of the edges and the low-confidence tier. Pruning them, the obvious hygiene move, lowers \textsc{systema} and depresses the gates; meanwhile a live-gate rationale audit puts nearly all sufficiency mass on exactly that tier ($\Delta=0.365$, against $\leq0.0021$ for BioPlex, HuRI and CORUM). The graph is therefore mostly noise with its signal in its least confident edges, so a model benefits only if it can switch edges off. Ours can, which is why it reaches parity rather than the static arm's deficit, but gating recovers only enough to break even.

The frozen feature vector that arm carries is graph-derived in two places: three PPI degree scalars, whose physical and functional components are nonzero for $96.5\%$ and $98.4\%$ of rows (medians $53$ and $604$), and a $128$-d PINNACLE embedding \citep{li2024pinnacle} learned on a cell-type-contextualized interaction network. \section{When a pre-registration freezes a mistake}
\label{app:prereg}

% AUTO 2026-08-19: moved here from Limitations for the page budget; content unchanged.
% AUTO 2026-08-26: this opened with the same seven words as App.~\ref{app:repl}. That section

\paragraph{The rule, and what it cost.} Under the original rule, before the amendment this section is
about, we built differential-expression matrices for six harmonized
public candidates \citep{peidli2024scperturb}; the corrected rule later built ten
(Table~\ref{tab:repl}). The
pre-registration fixed the differential-expression unit as ``two or more replicate pseudobulks per
(target, condition)'' at a $25$-cell floor, and specified that a dataset unable to meet it is dropped
rather than switched to a different test. Four candidates fell out. The clause did what such clauses
are for: when Replogle RPE1, the best-powered candidate available, yielded only $12$ of $2{,}393$
targets, we did not rescue it by splitting cells into random pseudo-replicates, which would have
manufactured precision we did not have.

\paragraph{The audit, and why the rule was wrong.} The clause was also wrong, and it took an audit
rather than a result to see it. The reference dataset's own supervised target is not built that way.
It is an artifact published with the source screen, computed by its authors over all cells for each
(perturbation, condition) against matched controls, with no per-replicate requirement at all. We had
imposed on every candidate replication a standard we never imposed on the data the entire study rests
on, and the consequence was to discard the best-powered replication available for failing a test the
reference itself would have failed. Under the reference's own standard, retaining the $25$-cell floor,
the same file yields $2{,}122$ targets rather than $12$.

\paragraph{The failure mode this illustrates.} Discussion of pre-registration in machine learning is
almost entirely about the failure mode it prevents: analytic flexibility exercised after seeing
results. It has a second, rarely mentioned. It requires committing an analysis plan at the point of
minimum understanding of the data, so a rule written then can be wrong in a way that stays invisible
until something forces an audit, and the commitment that makes pre-registration valuable is exactly
what makes such an error durable, because relaxing a pre-registered rule looks like the thing
pre-registration exists to prevent. Our defence is procedural rather than rhetorical: the amendment is
dated, it states that no replication arm had been trained when it was written, and it is
outcome-symmetric, admitting datasets that could support a positive replication as readily as a null.
Any pre-registration in this setting should include an explicit audit step that checks each rule
against how the reference data were themselves constructed, before the rule is frozen.

\paragraph{The null survives a harder split.} Lowering the sequence-similarity threshold from $0.85$ to $0.75$ and raising the family-size cap from $5\%$ to $15\%$ blocks more paralogs and lowers the median train-to-sequestered-split sequence cosine from $0.796$ to $0.741$. We compare only paired within-split contrasts across folds: the validation sets differ in size and composition, so absolute \textsc{systema} is not comparable between splits even when the contrast is. Section~\ref{sec:causes} explains why we do not treat that cosine as a clean difficulty axis either. The gated graph stays at parity ($\Delta\,\textsc{systema}=+0.0005$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0105,+0.0115]$, $p=0.90$, $n{=}5$ paired seeds, per-seed differences $-0.0003$, $+0.0035$, $+0.0032$, $-0.0138$, $+0.0100$). We note that the point estimate here is nominally \emph{positive}, unlike the frozen fold's $-0.0009$, and we do not read anything into that: the interval straddles zero almost symmetrically, the contrast clears neither correction, and one seed ($-0.0138$) moves the mean more than the estimate itself. The honest summary is that the sign is undetermined at this budget and the harder split gives a wider interval, not a tighter bound.

A third fold at an intermediate threshold gives the same corrected verdict everywhere (Table~\ref{tab:folds}); Appendix~\ref{app:power} records why we decline to read a difficulty trend into the point estimates.

We then measured why, and the answer is that these are not three difficulty levels. Holding threshold and cap fixed at the intermediate setting and varying only the partition seed, three realizations of the \emph{same} split specification give median train-to-sequestered-split cosines of $0.759$, $0.793$ and $0.862$. That is a spread of $0.103$ from partition noise alone, against $0.056$ for the entire designed range across all three thresholds ($0.796$ to $0.741$): \textbf{re-drawing one split moves the difficulty statistic almost twice as far as deliberately changing the threshold does}, and the third re-draw is \emph{easier} than the frozen fold it was supposed to sit below. The realizations also differ structurally, sharing only $20\%$ of their validation targets and differing twofold in validation size, and the no-graph baseline moves with them: at five paired seeds per re-draw it scores $0.0991$, $0.0942$ and $0.0845$ \textsc{systema}, a $0.0146$ spread on our most stable arm, larger than either effect in Table~\ref{tab:folds}. % AUTO 2026-08-26: "it tracks validation-set size" was a causal claim off three points, where a
% perfect rank match arises by chance one time in six. Stated as the association it is.
It does not track the cosine statistic either, since the nominally hardest re-draw scores highest. It orders with validation-set size, which the re-draws moved from $3{,}632$ to $7{,}216$ unbidden, though with three re-draws that ordering would arise by chance one time in six and we offer it as the more plausible reading rather than a measured relationship.

The contrast itself moves too, and this is the part we would most want from someone else's paper. Running the graph-versus-no-graph comparison on all three re-draws at five paired seeds each gives h1 $=+0.0082$ (uncorrected $95\%$ CI $[+0.0017,+0.0148]$, $p=0.025$), $+0.0026$ ($[+0.0005,+0.0047]$, $p=0.027$) and $+0.0021$ ($[-0.0024,+0.0065]$, $p=0.266$). Same experiment, same specification, same five seeds, differing only in how the partition was drawn: the estimate spans fourfold and \emph{the qualitative verdict flips}. Two re-draws give an interval excluding zero at $p\approx0.03$; the third does not. An author who drew the first would report an apparent graph benefit, one who drew the third would report nothing, and neither would be wrong. What survives all three is the sign and the correction, since none clears Bonferroni over the pre-registered family of four ($0.101$, $0.109$, $1.000$). That is why we let the corrected verdict carry the paper and treat the uncorrected intervals as the cautionary exhibit rather than the result.

The consequence is not that our contrasts are wrong, since each is paired within its own split, but that the \emph{ordering} in Table~\ref{tab:folds} carries far less information than three rows suggest. We therefore explicitly do \textbf{not} claim a dose-response in OOD difficulty, and we suggest the same caution for any single-realization split sweep: a threshold knob that appears to control difficulty may be moving it less than the seed does. \section{The detection floor, measured}
\label{app:floor}

The simulation in Appendix~\ref{app:power} says what this pipeline should be able to detect. This says
whether it does. We add a known graph-dependent component to the real responses at a ladder of sizes and
report the smallest one recovered. For each perturbation target the component is the mean response of its
protein-interaction neighbors, computed over \emph{training rows only}, scaled so its spread matches the
training response's and multiplied by $\delta$; so $\delta$ reads as a fraction of a response standard
deviation. A model that can read the graph can predict a held-out target from its neighbors. One that
cannot, cannot. Six conditions, two arms, four paired seeds each: $48$ training runs, no failures.

Two properties make the number mean anything, and both were fixed before any lane ran. No validation
response reaches any injected value, including through the scaling constant, asserted by a test that
perturbs a validation response and requires the whole injection matrix to be bit-identical. And one rung

largest $\delta$, preserving the injected component's size and distribution while destroying its relation
to the graph. Rows outside the training and validation folds are left bit-identical, so the sequestered
split is untouched.

\section{The null under other people's endpoints}
\label{app:metrics}

Outside results differ from ours in two ways at once, splits and metrics. The metric half is checkable
without retraining anything, because every arm's per-row predictions for the frozen validation fold are
persisted. We re-scored all four arms at all five seeds under five endpoints, verified from the papers
that report them rather than assumed: Pearson-$\Delta$ over all genes, which TxPert reports; Pearson
correlation and mean squared error over each perturbation's top-$20$ differentially expressed genes,
which \textsc{gears} reports \citep{roohani2024gears}; the E-distance as scPerturb computes it
\citep{peidli2024scperturb}; and the energy distance with plain Euclidean distances. The
differentially-expressed subset is taken from the \emph{observed} response, never the prediction.

Two cautions belong with the distance endpoints. First, scPerturb computes its E-distance from squared
Euclidean distances, and in that form the statistic collapses algebraically to
$2\lVert \bar{X}-\bar{Y}\rVert^2$: it is a difference of means, blind to a difference in spread, so we
report it for commensurability and treat the plain-Euclidean energy distance as the distributional
evidence. Second, this pipeline predicts one pseudobulk response per (target, condition), so both
distances compare the distribution of \emph{responses across held-out perturbations}, never cell
populations. Claims about single-cell distributions stay out of reach.

Five endpoints times four contrasts is twenty simultaneous tests, and correcting only within each
endpoint and then reporting whichever endpoint was kind is precisely the look-elsewhere error this
paper is about. We fixed the correction in the pre-registration before reading the fold: both bars are
computed, and a survival claim requires the across-metric one, Bonferroni and Holm together.
Table~\ref{tab:metrics} reports all twenty cells.

\paragraph{The crossover is a property of the cumulative statistic, not of where the graph starts
helping.} Every top-$k$ set contains all smaller ones, so a sign change in Table~\ref{tab:ksweep} only
bounds where the underlying per-gene effect turned. Re-scoring the same stored predictions on
\emph{disjoint} rank intervals, the increments between consecutive rows of that table, separate
the two (Table~\ref{tab:bins}). The untyped arm's deficit is confined to each perturbation's twenty
most-moved genes, where it is large ($-0.0424$), negative in $5/5$ seeds, and clears both corrections
over all $36$ cells. By rank $101$ the per-interval contrast is already positive in $5/5$ seeds, and
from rank $251$ down to the $10{,}282$nd gene every interval is positive and clears both corrections.
The cumulative crossover sits at $250$ to $500$ only because each running average carries the top-$20$
deficit forward until enough positive mass accumulates to cancel it; the per-gene effect turns between
rank $100$ and $250$. The honest reading of Table~\ref{tab:ksweep} is therefore not ``the graph helps
beyond the $500$th gene'' but ``the graph helps everywhere except the handful of genes each
perturbation moves hardest''.

Two cautions on reading Table~\ref{tab:bins}. First, the \emph{level} of a within-interval correlation
is not comparable across intervals: genes are selected by the same observed response that is the
correlation's target, so range restriction and selection on a noisy statistic both bias it. Only the
contrast within an interval is clean, because both arms are scored on the identical genes of the
identical rows and neither arm's prediction enters the selection. Second, the resolution of the binning
decides whether the harm is visible at all: repeating this at decile resolution hides it entirely, since
the top decile spans $1{,}028$ genes and the twenty damaged ones are diluted to a positive $+0.0064$
that clears nothing. A coarser analysis of the same predictions would have concluded that the graph
never hurts.
