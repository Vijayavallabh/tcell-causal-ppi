Does a typed protein-interaction prior improve prediction of how primary human T cells respond to genetic perturbation? We report two failures, and the more useful one is not the one we went looking for. The first is an instrument failure that nearly published itself: a textbook edge-sparsity regularizer, written as an unnormalized sum over the edges of each sampled neighborhood, is roughly 103x the response term at its default weight and drives every learnable edge gate to ~10^-7 inside the first epoch, in all five seeds. Training completes and reports a plausible score, so a graph-versus-no-graph comparison silently becomes no-graph versus no-graph. The second is the null itself. With the gates demonstrably live, the evidence-gated graph is indistinguishable from an expression-only baseline (delta SYSTEMA=-0.0009, 95% CI [-0.0072,+0.0054], n=5, family-wise corrected) and a typed static graph is reliably worse; that second, seemingly solid finding then failed its own replication, shrinking elevenfold to -0.0012 (p=0.61) on a harder split at the same five seeds, with one seed reversing sign. Rather than stop at the number we adjudicate five candidate causes against evidence we can produce: one is refuted, one holds partly, three survive, and two of the three are not about biology at all. Our baseline was never graph-free, since zeroing its graph-derived features costs -0.0036 SYSTEMA under correction, all of it three degree scalars, against the -0.0009 that message passing over eight million typed edges is worth on the same fold. And the arm we had never run overturned the reading: strip the typing and the gating, keep the raw topology, and that plain arm beats the baseline on our own screen (+0.0043, 95% CI [+0.0017,+0.0069], n=7, surviving Bonferroni and Holm) and on Replogle RPE1 (+0.0675), and loses to it on Norman (-0.0790). Three of eight datasets clear correction in disagreeing directions (I^2=87.5%, pooled random effects +0.0091, [-0.0024,+0.0207]), while the typed null holds and tightens across seven further screens (pooled +0.0018, random-effects 95% CI [-0.0028,+0.0065], I^2=39%, Q p=0.13). So the prior is not what fails; the encoder we built to exploit it is, and a "graph versus no graph" null can be an artifact of the graph encoder rather than evidence about the graph. Our best graph arm recovers an injected graph signal at 0.02 response SDs and recovers nothing when the same signal follows a scrambled graph. The gated arm our null is stated on clears at no injected size up to 0.40, at up to 9x the paired noise, and on the increment isolating the injection neither arm separates below 0.40. That null bounds what this encoder can resolve at four seeds rather than whether the prior helps. We close with the checklist we wish we had used.
